\begin{abstract}
Populations of automatically generated LLM harnesses are increasingly used for
routing and value prediction --- applications that presuppose population members
\emph{disagree} on outcomes. We audit this premise. In a Phase-I discovery study on
BIRD text-to-SQL, a standard harness-evolution population proved syntactically diverse
yet behaviorally collapsed: 36 of 91 harness pairs returned identical correctness on
every task, code similarity showed no observed rank association with outcome
disagreement, and execution-trace auditing traced the failure to mechanisms that were
described but never executed. A hand-written positive control established that the
routing headroom exists on the task (7.3\,pp) --- the failure was on the generation
side. We then froze a confirmatory protocol: a $2{	imes}2$ factorial (strategy
forcing $	imes$ counterfactual conformance gate) over six builders and three seeds,
evaluated on 1{,}169 questions from databases never opened during development, under
the official BIRD scorer. The confirmatory results revise the story twice over.
First, \emph{collapse proved protocol-sensitive}: under the clean equal-budget
protocol, free ungated generation already produced headroom-rich populations
(8.6\,pp oracle headroom, 31\% union repair) --- the collapse regime did not
reproduce. Second, the combined intervention showed \emph{no clear improvement}
($-0.16$\,pp, 95\% CI $[-0.90, +0.82]$), and the apparent negative effect of the
conformance gate was substantially size-confounded: at matched population size it is
not distinguishable from zero ($-0.30$\,pp, CI $[-0.73, +0.11]$), and the API
nondeterminism noise floor (14.5\% bare-outcome flip rate at temperature 0) exceeds
the sub-part-per-point effects under interpretation. The stable conclusion is a
measurement mandate: syntactic diversity is not population utility; report outcome
disagreement, repair, harm, and headroom before routing over generated populations ---
and audit generation protocols, since collapse emerged from a deployed pipeline that
had passed every code-level check.
\end{abstract}
