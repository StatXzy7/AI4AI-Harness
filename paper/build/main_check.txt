A BSTRACT
Populations of automatically generated LLM harnesses underpin routing and
value prediction, which presuppose that population members disagree on outcomes. We audit this premise on BIRD text-to-SQL. A Phase-I discovery study
found a standard harness-evolution population syntactically diverse yet behaviorally collapsed — 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on every task, code similarity
unassociated with outcome disagreement — and traced the failure by executiontrace audit to mechanisms described but never executed. A hand-written positive control showed the routing headroom exists (7.3 pp); the failure was on
the generation side. A pre-specified confirmatory factorial study (strategy forcing × conformance gate; six builders, three seeds; 1,169 questions from neveropened databases; official BIRD scorer) then revised the story twice. First, collapse proved protocol-sensitive: under a clean equal-budget protocol, free ungated generation already produced headroom-rich populations (8.6 pp oracle headroom, 31% union repair). Second, the intervention showed no clear improvement (−0.16 pp, CI [−0.90, +0.82]); the apparent negative gate effect was substantially size-confounded (−0.30 pp at matched K, CI spanning zero) and the
bare-outcome noise floor (14.5% flip rate at temperature 0) exceeds the effects in
question. The stable conclusion is a measurement mandate: report outcome disagreement, repair, harm, and headroom before routing over generated populations
— and audit the generation protocol, since the collapse we found was invisible to
every code-level check the pipeline had passed.

I NTRODUCTION

The fastest-growing lever on a frozen LLM is no longer weights but the harness — the program of
prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps the model for a task. A growing line of “AI4AI” systems
generates this scaffolding automatically: LLM builders propose, edit, and evolve harness code. The
implicit premise is that when the generated code differs, the behavior differs — that a population
of thirty harnesses is thirty ways of answering, not one way written thirty times. Routing and value
prediction over such populations presuppose exactly this.
Phase I (discovery): the premise fails, and for identifiable reasons. On BIRD text-to-SQL (Li
et al., 2023) with a frozen mid-tier target, we measure what the candidates of a standard harnessevolution loop actually do, not what their source files say (Figure 1). Every candidate passes a
code-difference check, yet among the 12 generated candidates (66 candidate pairs) average pairwise outcome disagreement is 3.8% and 21 pairs are behaviorally identical; counting the shipped
react harness and bare baseline as well (91 pairs), 36 pairs return identical correctness on every
task, and pairwise code similarity shows no observed rank association with outcome disagreement
(Spearman ρ = −0.01). The shipped react variant reproduces bare’s final SQL byte-for-byte
on every audited task. Execution-trace auditing resolves “different code” into three failure classes:
mechanisms described but absent from the control flow, feedback with no causal path to the output,
and genuinely multi-turn paths that are broken (§3). We call this behavioral collapse.
Collapse is not a property of the task: four hand-written harnesses with forcibly distinct execution
paths deliver 7.3 pp oracle headroom on the same target and judge — the headroom routers need
is real; the generation side fails to populate it. We also document a second, more primitive failure
mode in one deployed pipeline: a widely used optimize loop emitted candidates byte-identical to

Phase II (confirmatory): a pre-specified factorial. Phase I suggested that behavioral collapse can

development items, and its judge was a conservative proxy. We therefore froze a confirmatory protocol before any confirmatory outcome was observed: a 2×2 factorial over strategy forcing (assigned
mechanism vs. free invention) and a mechanism-conformance gate (counterfactual trace verification vs. neutral validity only), crossed with six builder models and three seeds, evaluated on 1,169
questions from nine databases never opened during development, scored by the official BIRD judge,
with the primary contrast (forced+gated vs. free+ungated populations) analyzed as paired continuous effects with hierarchical bootstrap intervals (§4). The conformance instruments are calibrated
against hand-written positive controls and known-negative impostor harnesses before any candidate
is scored.
The confirmatory answer revises the story twice: the Phase-I collapse did not reproduce under
the clean protocol (free generation was already headroom-rich), and the intervention itself was
null within measurement precision — the apparent negative gate effect was substantially sizeconfounded and not Holm-significant, and the API nondeterminism noise floor exceeds the sub-pp
effects in question (Section 5).
Our contributions:
1. An outcome-matrix measurement of behavioral collapse in AI-generated harness populations, with an execution-trace taxonomy explaining why code diversity fails to become
behavioral diversity, and a clean separation between genuine collapse and generation-null
failures.
2. Calibrated generation-side instruments and a pre-specified factorial study: a counterfactual conformance suite and self-specified behavioral contracts, both calibrated on positive and negative controls; a 2×2 design with equal raw generation budgets that isolates
the combined intervention and its components on uncontaminated data under the official
scorer.
3. A boundary result for routing: on the Phase-I populations, open-set harness-value prediction does not beat the best fixed harness, and its apparent per-task signal is consistent
with task memorization; we scope this precisely to the tested predictors and splits (§5.3).

2.2

H ARNESS CONDITIONAL UTILITY AND ROUTING

Several recent papers establish that harness quality is conditional on the instance: Gupta et al. (2026)
and the non-monotonicity analysis of Cho (2026) document that no single harness dominates across
instances. HELIX (Fan & Huang, 2026) shows a portfolio of sibling harnesses beats the best fixed
one and lists a harness router as future work; STS (Zhou et al., 2026) routes over six fixed humandesigned scaffolds in a closed set; GRASP (Moll et al., 2026) gates skill edits into a library at
development time; Kim (2026) learn counterfactual instance-level routing over {no-search, search,
abstain}; Liu et al. (2026) route models within a harness. All of these consume a population whose
members actually disagree; Fan & Huang (2026) comes closest, evaluating a full candidate outcome
matrix and measuring portfolio complementarity beyond the best fixed candidate. Our contribution
is narrower and different: we measure whether automatically generated populations collapse as an
outcome phenomenon, diagnose why at the trace level, and isolate the generation-side causes with a
controlled factorial.
2.3

B EHAVIORAL DIVERSITY IN LLM POPULATIONS

Diversity of LLM outputs has mostly been studied at the sample level (self-consistency, diverse
decoding) or the agent-policy level. The observation that syntactically distinct programs can exhibit
identical behavior echoes coverage gaps documented in program synthesis and mutation-testing
practice; our contribution is to instantiate and quantify it for AI-generated harness populations, with
a mechanism-level taxonomy.

P HASE I: M EASURING AND D IAGNOSING THE C OLLAPSE (D ISCOVERY )

Phase I is a discovery study: its role is to establish the phenomenon, the taxonomy, and the existence of task-side headroom, and to motivate the confirmatory design. None of its results is confirmatory: 24 of its 151 evaluation items overlapped protocol-development measurements, the human

(Appendix A). We report it with those limits stated.

3.2

T HE COLLAPSE , QUANTIFIED

The domain is BIRD text-to-SQL (SQLite; execution-accuracy judging), target frozen at GLM-5.3Flash (temperature 0) throughout Phase I. On the day-1 population — 14 harnesses (bare, react,
and 12 evolution candidates) × 60 tasks — average pairwise outcome disagreement is 3.3% and 36
of 91 pairs are identical on every task; among the 12 generated candidates alone (66 candidate
pairs; mean pairwise code similarity 0.143 under the normalized-character metric of the original
code-difference check), disagreement is 3.8% with 21 identical pairs. Every candidate had passed a
code-difference check (“all files distinct, non-trivial diffs”). Union repair over bare errors is 7.7%
and oracle headroom 3.3 pp; react differs from bare on zero tasks.
Figure 1 makes the central relation quantitative: across all 91 pairs, pairwise source-code similarity
under the figure’s token-sequence metric (mean 0.190; distinct from the candidates-only normalizedcharacter metric of 0.143 above) has Spearman ρ = −0.010 with outcome disagreement — code
similarity shows no observed rank association with behavioral similarity in this population. (Pairs
share harnesses and are not independent samples; we report ρ as a descriptive summary, not an
inferential test.)

T RACE TAXONOMY: WHY CODE DIVERSITY FAILS TO BECOME BEHAVIORAL DIVERSITY

• T1 — mechanism described, not executed (6/12 candidates). The harness issues exactly one LLM call and never executes an artifact — the identical execution path as bare.
Docstrings claim retrieval, self-checking, or token caps; none exists in the control flow.
• T2 — feedback without a causal path (react, 10/10 audited tasks). SQL is executed
and errors fed back, yet the final SQL is byte-identical to bare’s on every audited task.
• T3 — broken multi-turn path (1/12). The one candidate implementing genuine multiturn repair returns prose as final SQL, converting bare-correct items into harness-wrong.
The taxonomy is actionable: T1 demands an execution-path acceptance gate, T2 demands verified
causal paths for feedback, T3 demands executable-output validation — all generation-protocol requirements.
3.4

H UMAN POSITIVE CONTROL : THE HEADROOM EXISTS

To exclude the alternative that the domain simply lacks instance-conditional signal, we wrote
four harnesses by hand with forcibly distinct execution paths (hpc repair: execute-and-repair;
hpc vote3: 3-vote on execution results; hpc schema: schema-linking; hpc hint: hint-first
reordering). Same frozen target, tasks, and judge as the collapsed population: oracle headroom
6.67 pp on 60 tasks and 7.28 pp [3.3, 10.0] on the 151-item set, with repair/schema/hint each producing repairs the others miss. One disclosed caveat: the control was designed after inspecting
day-1 traces, so its headroom is an existence proof, not a blind estimate. The bare baseline itself
flipped 3 of 60 items between the two day-1 runs (API nondeterminism at temperature 0).
3.5

P HASE -I PROTOCOL PROBES AND THEIR LIMITS

Phase I also probed generation-side fixes — a strategy-forced free-form protocol (assigned mechanism, import/smoke acceptance) and a compiled Harness IR. Under the strategy-forced protocol
two of three builders produced populations whose point estimates exceeded the pre-specified thresholds (Qwen3.8-Flash 5.96, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 7.95 pp; the old-protocol arm missed at
4.64 pp), and per-strategy acceptance ordered with builder identity (GLM-5.3-Flash 3/8, Qwen3.8Flash 7/8, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 8/8, under the logged tool). These observations motivate
Phase II but are not confirmatory, for three disclosed reasons: the design was two nested contrasts
that cannot separate protocol from builder main effects; the early runs lack per-attempt logs, so budget equality cannot be audited; and acceptance was an import/smoke check, not the counterfactual
conformance gate of §4. Full condition details are in Appendix A.
Generation-null failures (M0). Re-running the original optimize loop with Qwen3.8-Flash and
DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp builders produced 40 candidates byte-identical to the bare baseline source. Identical code trivially yields identical outcomes, so this run provides no evidence
about behavioral collapse; it is reported as a distinct, more primitive failure mode — the pipeline
produced no distinct population at all. The collapse claims above rest exclusively on the genuinely
distinct-source day-1 population.

D ESIGN

Equal raw generation budget. Every slot in every arm receives exactly R=3 raw builder attempts
(same model, temperature 0.7, 16,384 token cap). Arms differ only in which raw candidate is admitted: ungated arms take the first candidate passing neutral validity (parses, imports, returns SQL
on a development question — mechanism-blind); gated arms take the first additionally passing the
conformance suite. Slot failure is final; populations are never topped up, so generation reliability
is part of the treatment. All raw candidates are retained and generation reliability is reported separately from population quality via three rates: Rartifact (valid artifact), Rcontract (valid, non-vacuous
contract), Rfidelity (asserted properties verified, conditional on a valid contract).
Builders and seeds. Six builders from independent labs (GLM-5.3, Qwen3.8-Max, DeepSeekV4-Pro, Kimi-K3, MiniMax-M3, ERNIE-5.0-Thinking-Preview), three seeds each: 18 paired
(builder × seed) cells. These were the strongest models available on the project’s single API gateway; no Anthropic- or OpenAI-family builder was accessible, so frontier capability-dependence is
untested. Every cell enters the primary contrast regardless of admitted population size, including
K=0 — excluding low-yield cells would condition on a post-treatment outcome.
Data isolation. A fail-closed contamination registry scans every project artifact for observed
BIRD questions. Two databases touched during development are banned wholesale; the confirmatory evaluation uses 1,169 questions from nine databases never opened during development
(the registry’s defensible claim is no project-specific adaptive use, not pretraining-time isolation; the
18-item smoke contact is the disclosed exception), with a stratified 400-item core drawn before any
outcome existed. Factorial secondary analyses run on the same core items for all four arms.
Judge. Primary: the official BIRD execution scorer (set equality on raw result rows, 30 s wallclock timeout). Secondary: the Phase-I legacy judge, measured to be uniformly conservative (on
Phase-I matrices it never credits an answer the official scorer rejects).
Instruments. The conformance suite drives each harness against scripted scenarios and asserts on
the recorded execution trace, never on task performance, so gating cannot leak outcome supervision.
Every mechanism is probed with paired counterfactual variants whose correct answer differs — including a variant where the first query succeeds, separating conditional repair from an unconditional
second call — and answers are compared by exact normalized equality. The suite was calibrated
before any Phase-II generation against three hand-written positive controls (all must pass) and two
known-negative impostor harnesses (both must be rejected), plus the invariant that the bare baseline
must violate every conditional property; the calibration is an executable gate re-run before each generation batch. Arm II-E additionally enforces non-vacuity (the contract must be violated by the bare
baseline, checked by running it) and verifies only the properties actually asserted.

FACTORIAL DECOMPOSITION ( SAME CORE -400 ITEMS )

The factorial decomposition must be read with two corrections the frozen plan anticipated and the
data now supply.
Size confounding. The gate admits ∼30% fewer candidates (69 vs. C’s 101 slots), and oracle headroom grows with population size, so part of the unmatched −0.67 pp is mechanical. At matched
population size (subsampling each ungated population to its gated arm’s K, 200 draws), the gate
main effect shrinks to −0.30 pp, 95% CI [−0.73, +0.11] — not distinguishable from zero. More
than half the unmatched effect was population size.
Multiplicity. The three factorial contrasts form a pre-specified Holm family. Raw permutation p
for the gate main effect is 0.028, but the Holm-adjusted p is 0.084 — not confirmatory even before
K-matching. The unmatched CI excluding zero was never pre-specified significance.
Noise floor. Three independent cache-off repeats of bare (R3, temperature 0) flip 14.5% of task
outcomes at least once (pairwise ∼9.7–10.0%; bare accuracy varies 63.8–65.8% across repeats).
The cache-off arm replication (R2; arms A and D, 70 harnesses, 28,000 core cells) flips 8.5% of
verdicts against the cached run while leaving arm-level disagreement structure and every subset
contrast unchanged (Appendix B); same-harness rerun disagreement is of the same order as crossharness disagreement, so a substantial share of measured pairwise diversity is run noise. The sub-pp
effects under interpretation sit an order of magnitude below this floor.

5.3

D IVERSITY DOES NOT IMPLY LEARNABLE ROUTING

Open-set value prediction does not beat the best fixed harness. A leave-one-harness-out predictor over the Phase-I admitted populations fails to beat the best fixed harness (DeepSeek-V4-FlashVision-Exp arm −3.4 pp, Qwen3.8-Flash +0.9 pp, CI [−5.2, +6.9]; cross-protocol pool −0.9 pp).
Apparent per-fold repair AUROC of 0.82–0.98 collapses under database-stratified task-held-out controls (mean 0.512) — the signal is task memorization, not harness-value estimation. One narrow
positive survives: as an abstaining gate on the 24-harness cross-protocol pool, the same predictor
gained +6.9 pp over bare with zero observed induced harm — on the same tasks the gate was tuned
on, so not a deployment claim. Selective abstention may be easier than full routing; estimating an
unseen harness’s per-item value from code and task text alone remains open.

6

D ISCUSSION AND C ONCLUSION

Protocol sensitivity is the finding. The Phase-I collapse did not reproduce under the clean equalbudget protocol: free ungated generation already produced populations with 8.6 pp oracle headroom
and 31% union repair. Collapse is a failure mode of specific generation pipelines, invisible to every
code-level check they passed. Adding mechanism-verification did not add routing value, and its
apparent cost was largely a population-size artifact: outcome-level auditing, not stronger code-level
gating, is what detects collapse. Sub-pp generation-side effects sit below the repeat-run noise floor
(14.5% bare any-flip), which belongs beside every CI. The yield and headroom decompositions show
the gate changed which candidates survive, not whether the population can answer: reliability and
complementarity need separate reporting.Limitations. One domain family (text-to-SQL); Phase-I
from a discovery design with disclosed task overlap, a proxy judge, and a post-trace human control;
inference conditional on the six sampled builders; early Phase-I generations lack per-attempt logs.
AI-generated harness populations can be exactly what their source files suggest and nothing like
what routing needs — and the reverse can also hold. Phase I measured the syntactic–behavioral gap
in a discovery population, traced its mechanisms, and showed the missing headroom exists on the
task. A pre-specified confirmatory factorial study (six builders, three seeds, 2×2 forcing × gate,
1,169 unseen questions) then revised the story twice. Collapse proved protocol-sensitive: a failure
mode of specific pipelines, invisible to every code-level check they passed. The intervention was
null within measurement precision: no primary-contrast improvement; the apparent negative gate
effect size-confounded and not Holm-significant; the 14.5% bare and 8.5% cache-off noise floors
exceed the sub-pp effects in question. The practical output stands: measure disagreement, repair,
harm, and headroom before routing, and audit the generation protocol itself.
