The confirmatory answer to our primary question is negative: the strategy-forced,
conformance-gated protocol does not increase population headroom relative to free,
ungated generation. The point estimate is small ($-0.16$\,pp), the hierarchical
bootstrap CI comfortably spans zero, and the permutation test offers no evidence
against the null. Per the pre-registered conditional conclusions, we report this as
\emph{no clear improvement} --- an unresolved estimate, not evidence of no effect.

Two secondary observations qualify the null. First, both arms carry \emph{substantial}
headroom (A: 3.1--11.7\,pp across cells; D: 4.5--11.9\,pp): the Phase-I collapse
regime is far from the clean-protocol regime, where free generation alone already
produces headroom-rich populations at these builder strengths. Second, the per-cell
deltas are heterogeneous ($-2.5$ to $+2.8$\,pp): small-K cells where the gate admits
harnesses the free arm lacks trend positive (deepseek/s1 $+2.0$, glm/s0 $+2.7$),
while cells where the free arm is already large trend negative --- consistent with the
substitution effect decomposed next. Notably, arm D repairs marginally fewer bare
errors (30.0\% vs.\ 31.3\%) while inducing more harm (24.6\% vs.\ 19.8\%): the
gate's fidelity filtering does not translate into gentler interventions.

\caption{\textbf{Confirmatory secondaries}, 18 quad cells, all four arms on the
identical 400-item core. The gate main effect is the only contrast whose CI excludes
zero --- on the negative side.}
\label{tab:factorial}
\end{table}

The factorial decomposition explains the primary null mechanistically:

\textbf{The conformance gate reduces headroom on its own} ($-0.67$\,pp, CI excluding
zero). Gate-rejected candidates --- neutral-valid code whose declared mechanism did not
survive counterfactual probing --- were, on this benchmark, net contributors to
population headroom. Mechanism fidelity and population complementarity trade against
each other: a harness that executes exactly what it says can still be behaviorally
redundant, while a sloppier candidate can repair items the clean ones miss.

\textbf{Strategy forcing alone is null} ($+0.41$\,pp, CI spans zero), and
\textbf{the interaction is negative} ($-0.87$\,pp, CI spans zero): gate and forcing
\emph{substitute} rather than complement. Whatever diversity forcing adds, the gate's
filter removes; the two effects largely cancel in the combined arm D --- which is
precisely the primary null.

Reliability and quality are different axes, and the design separates them. The gate's
D-arm selectivity is visible in the yield table: at nearly identical artifact rates,
the gate admits 69 of C's 101 slots. Combined with the headroom decomposition, the
consistent picture is that mechanism-conformance filtering costs both yield and
headroom on this benchmark.

\subsection{Robustness audits}\label{sec:results-audits}

Dual-judge agreement was measured on every cell (the legacy judge never credits an
answer the official scorer rejects). Concurrent-duplicate collection produced conflicting
verdicts on 1.8\% of AD cells and 1.4\% of BC cells; these are resolved
first-write-wins in canonical file order, disclosed in the completeness report, and
bounded: re-resolving every conflict the other way changes 0.84\% of cell verdicts,
which does not move any reported contrast. Cache-off replication and repeat-run
stability (R2/R3) are pending; they are reported when complete.
