AI-generated harness populations can be exactly what their source files
suggest and nothing like what routing needs. We measured that gap on a
genuinely distinct-source population --- code similarity carries no
information about outcome disagreement --- explained its mechanism at the
trace level, and showed the missing headroom exists on the task. We
separated genuine collapse from generation-null failures, and froze a
confirmatory factorial study that isolates the generation-side factors
under uncontaminated evaluation.

The Phase-II conclusion will be selected from the following
pre-drafted statements according to the frozen analysis, without
reference to which is most flattering:
\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item \textbf{If the primary contrast is positive with CI excluding
zero:} explicit strategy assignment plus counterfactual conformance
gating increases the routing-relevant headroom of generated harness
populations under equal raw budgets; the factorial decomposition then
locates the effect (gate, forcing, or interaction).
\item \textbf{If the contrast is near zero:} the combined intervention
does not increase population headroom on this benchmark; Phase-I's
positive point estimates were then protocol- or sample-specific, and the
collapse is not repaired by these generation-side interventions.
\item \textbf{If the contrast is negative:} the gated-forced protocol
reduces population headroom (e.g.\ by concentrating quality or costing
generation yield); the failure mode itself is informative for
harness-generation design.
\end{itemize}
In all cases the practical output stands: measure disagreement, union
repair, and headroom before routing over a generated population; gate
generation on verified execution, not on text.
