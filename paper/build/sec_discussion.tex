\textbf{The precondition metrics as a community tool.} The three metrics
of \S\ref{sec:metrics} are computable from the outcome matrix any
evaluation already produces. We argue they should be reported alongside
accuracy wherever populations of generated harnesses (or agents, or
scaffolds) are consumed by a selector. One caution the definition itself
supplies: failing an operational threshold does not mean routing is
impossible --- a population with 4\,pp of headroom fails a 5\,pp checklist
yet could still support a router that beats the best fixed option by up to
4\,pp. The checklist tells you which regime you are in; it is a
measurement convention, not a theorem about what a router can recover.

\textbf{Implications for AI4AI generation.} The taxonomy implies concrete
protocol requirements: acceptance defined on \emph{traces}, not docstrings
or code diffs; feedback mechanisms with a demonstrated causal path; and
executable-output validation before any capability claim. Phase II tests
exactly these requirements under controlled conditions: its gate verifies
mechanism execution via counterfactual probes, and its factorial design
can attribute effects to the gate, to strategy forcing, and to their
interaction, rather than to end-to-end system differences.

\textbf{Reliability and quality are different axes.} Phase-I observations
suggested builder identity governs generation reliability (3/8, 7/8, 8/8
strategies accepted) more than per-strategy solution quality; Phase II
makes this decomposition explicit by reporting $R_{\mathrm{artifact}}$,
$R_{\mathrm{contract}}$, and $R_{\mathrm{fidelity}}$ separately from
population headroom, under equal raw budgets. A protocol that reliably
produces nothing and one that produces diverse-but-redundant populations
are different failures, and the design can tell them apart.

\textbf{Limitations.} One domain family (text-to-SQL); Phase-I results
come from a discovery design with disclosed task overlap and a proxy
judge, and the human control was designed after inspecting development
traces. Phase-II results were pending at the time this draft was
assembled and will be reported under the frozen analysis plan without
reference to their direction. The routing boundary result is established
on the tested predictors, tasks, and splits only. Early Phase-I
generations lack per-attempt logs (disclosed in
Appendix~\ref{app:phase1-provenance}); Phase II closes this gap for all
of its runs.
