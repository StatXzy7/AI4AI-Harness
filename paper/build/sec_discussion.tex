\textbf{Protocol sensitivity is the finding.} The confirmatory study's central
result is that the Phase-I collapse did not reproduce under a clean equal-budget
protocol: free ungated generation already produced populations with 8.6\,pp oracle
headroom and 31\% union repair. Collapse is therefore a failure mode of
\emph{specific} generation pipelines --- and it was invisible to every code-level
check those pipelines had passed (distinct files, non-trivial diffs, import and
smoke tests). The practical corollary cuts against the natural instinct: adding
mechanism-verification did not add routing value here, and its apparent cost was
largely a population-size artifact. Outcome-level auditing, not stronger
code-level gating, is what detects collapse.

\textbf{Sub-part-per-point effects need a noise floor.} The bare-outcome
flip rate under API nondeterminism (14.5\% any-flip across three temperature-0
repeats) exceeds the effect sizes the factorial estimates ($-0.67$\,pp unmatched,
$-0.30$\,pp K-matched). We therefore decline to interpret the residual. For
studies of generation-side interventions, reporting the repeat-run noise floor
alongside every contrast should be as standard as reporting the CIs themselves.

\textbf{Reliability and quality are different axes.} The yield decomposition
(gate rejects $\sim$30\% of neutral-valid candidates) and the headroom
decomposition (oracle accuracy essentially unchanged across arms) must be read
together: the gate changed \emph{which} candidates survive, not \emph{whether}
the population can answer. Generation reliability and population complementarity
need separate reporting.

\textbf{Limitations.} One domain family (text-to-SQL). Phase-I results come
from a discovery design with disclosed task overlap, a proxy judge, and a human
control designed after inspecting development traces. Inference on the factorial
is conditional on the six sampled builders. The cache-off arm replication (R2)
is in progress; early Phase-I generations lack per-attempt logs.
