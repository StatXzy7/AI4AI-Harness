\section{Statistical Analysis, Calibration, and Sensitivity}\label{app:statistics}\label{app:diagnostics}
This section expands the held-out-repeat diagnostic in the main text.
Historical archive applications and their separate data splits are
retained in Appendix~\ref{app:archive-analysis}.
\subsection{Decision rule and post-collection analysis revisions}
The primary complementarity statistic is the cross-fitted
frozen-selection gain $G$ of \S\ref{sec:calibration-test}: in each of
three folds, two repeats freeze the per-task member mapping and the best
\emph{fixed} member, the third repeat scores the frozen mapping against
that fixed member; $G$ is the per-task mean averaged over folds.
Tie-breaking is label-symmetric: because $R{=}2$ discovery scores lie
on $\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$ and ties dominate at high accuracy, a deterministic
lexicographic rule would bias the selection toward the first listed
member, so iid $U(0,10^{-9})$ jitter is drawn per (fold, task) and
averaged over ten frozen tie realizations; a simulation over
dominant-member positions verifies the statistic is order-independent.
The decision statistic is the real-minus-clone difference
$D=G_{\rm real}-G_{\rm clone}$ on the task intersection complete in both
arms. Its reference distribution uses $10{,}000$ two-arm randomizations:
within every task and repeat, member labels are permuted independently
in each arm and $D$ is recomputed (a single permutation joint across
repeats was rejected in development because it preserves a genuine
crossover mapping and does not null it). A paired task bootstrap
(2{,}000 draws with a fixed random seed) gives the 95\% interval. The frozen rule
emits SUPPORTED only when the coverage gate passes ($\le10\%$ incomplete
tasks in either arm and on their intersection; $\ge30$ paired tasks), the randomization $p<0.05$,
the bootstrap lower bound is positive, $D>1.0$\,\%, and $G_{\rm real}$'s
own lower bound is positive; REFUTED requires the $D$ upper bound within
the margin and a small equivalence $p$; otherwise INSUFFICIENT. The
acceptance rule that any of these states terminates the study without
re-running remains. The earlier v2 procedure --- a discovery-repeat
argmax scored against \bare{} on repeats 2--3 (A8.6) --- is retained as
a labeled secondary statistic, not a complementarity test (under pure
global dominance with \bare{} at 0.5 and the other members at 0.9 its
expectation is $\approx0.5(1-0.1^8)\cdot0.4\approx0.20$). Two frozen
sensitivity branches accompany it (within-task available-repeat mean
imputation; exclusion of doubly-executed conflict cells).

\subsection{Diagnostic targets and calibration} For a population of harnesses and a single-call
reference, the diagnostics pipeline evaluates five classes of claims on an
explicit execution matrix $Y[h,x,r,c]$ (member, task, repeat, execution
condition): \textbf{(A)} measurement sufficiency (manifest consistency,
judge replay with an explicit executed/not-executed state, identity
uniqueness; a check that was not run is reported as not-run and can never
render the class SUPPORTED); \textbf{(B)} single-shot coverage (oracle minus
best-fixed, with dev-fixed reported separately); \textbf{(C)} stability, in
two separated estimands; \textbf{(D)} pre-execution selectability (a frozen
one-call selection policy versus dev-fixed); \textbf{(E)} budget-matched policy
utility under a frozen 1-call budget with $\lambda{=}1$ harm weighting
(offline, one-call; E leaves INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE unless the per-task call
budget is verified by per-record evidence or is part of the generating
design). Each
class returns SUPPORTED, REFUTED\_WITHIN\_SCOPE, INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE, or
NOT\_APPLICABLE with scope and minimal missing evidence; classes are never
collapsed into a single score.

\paragraph{Two stability estimands.} \emph{C-rank}: pairwise same-condition
accuracy differences with a SHARED task-resample bootstrap and max-T
simultaneous 95\% intervals over all $M(M-1)/2$ canonical member pairs
(family-wise error control; the pre-v3 per-pair intervals did not adjust
for 36 simultaneous comparisons), plus sign consistency across repeats;
the pair direction is fixed by member identity, so population conclusions
are invariant to member ordering. The population state means
\emph{at least one} pair differs after multiplicity adjustment, not that
the complete ranking is stable. \emph{C-comp}: for a general population
stable complementarity is $H_{\rm stable}=\mathbb{E}\max_h q_h(x)-\max_h\mathbb{E}
q_h(x)$, zero exactly when one member is expectation-best on every task;
for two members this reduces to the half-absolute-value form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:identity}, which is the only form displayed in the main
text. Repeats with differing
execution-condition fields or differing program identities are refused
as matched repeats and force INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE; byte-identical
outcome matrices are refused unless both repeats carry distinct,
explicitly independent execution provenance (legitimate
near-deterministic programs can reproduce identical matrices, so
identity equality triggers provenance verification rather than an
automatic rejection). Pair-level imputation bounds over unknown cells
are reported alongside the recorded success rate.

\paragraph{The revised C-comp decision.}
The repeat-averaged plug-in of $H_{\rm stable}$ is retained as a
\emph{descriptive} field only; it never determines a scientific state.
Two known-truth counterexamples from the review are encoded as
regression tests: all $q=0.9$ with $M{=}9,T{=}400,R{=}3$ gives a plug-in
$\widehat H=8.69$\,\% (the mean over the $300$ fixed simulated
datasets), and the
earlier available-repeat clone estimate is $2.67$\,\%
(the complete-task estimand is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:wp1r}) --- both with true
$H_{\rm stable}=0$. The calibrated statistic is the cross-fitted
frozen-selection gain $G$ described in \S\ref{sec:calibration-test}; the
decision uses the real-minus-clone difference $D$.

\emph{What holds at the population level, and what is checked
empirically.} Let
$\theta^*$ be a selector's population gain relative to the
population-best fixed member. On every task the selected member's probability is at most
$\max_h q_h(x)$, so $\theta^*\le H_{\rm stable}$. The null $H_{\rm
stable}=0$ is the global-dominance cone: some $h_0$ satisfies
$q_{h_0}(x)\ge q_h(x)$ for all $h$ and almost every $x$. Under dominance
a population-optimal mapping routes to the dominant member, so
the population gain is $\theta^*\le0$ throughout the cone, not just at
equal ability. This compares with the \emph{population-best fixed}
member, whereas the finite-$(R,T)$ statistic $G$ uses a fixed member
selected from discovery data. In that statistic, the discovery argmax
is selected from noisy repeats, and a weaker member that ties the noisy
discovery score can be chosen in a finite sample (the sealed clone arm
prints $G_{\rm clone}=0.55$\,\% under finite-sample variation),
so $\theta^*\le0$ does not by itself bound the finite-sample rejection
probability. The two-arm Monte Carlo randomization reference is exact at
the equal-ability boundary, where permuted labels are exchangeable and
the recomputed statistic (with the $+1$ correction) is a draw from the
same conditional distribution. Over the rest of the dominance cone,
false support is verified \emph{empirically} on every null DGP in the
calibration grid below --- including heterogeneous difficulty,
heterogeneous dominance, the $0.5/0.9$ configuration, missingness, and
sticky within-task latent states matched between arms --- rather than
asserted as an analytic finite-sample result. A level-$.05$ procedure can
false-support at level $.05$ on a finite grid; the defensible claim is
the observed worst rate and its Wilson interval, not impossibility.

\emph{Tie-breaking.} Member-label tie-breaking in the frozen argmax is
iid $U(0,10^{-9})$ jitter averaged over ten frozen tie realizations on
the real-data report (each calibration dataset uses one draw: under the
null the tie bias has mean zero); a deterministic lexicographic rule is
not label-invariant and is rejected by simulation. Label-symmetry is
asserted directly by a permutation-invariance regression test: the
10-draw tie-averaged $G$, $D$, and state agree up to Monte Carlo
tolerance when the member order is permuted identically in both arms.

\paragraph{Randomization mechanics.} Labels are permuted independently
within every task AND every repeat (a single joint permutation across
repeats preserves a genuine crossover mapping and does not null the
statistic: verified in development), recomputing both $G$'s, their
difference, and the two selections on every draw; the $+1$ Monte Carlo
correction gives an exact discrete level. A paired task bootstrap
supplies the sampling interval; a $\le10\%$ incomplete-task coverage
gate, extremal unknown-cell bounds, and a positive $G_{\rm real}$ sign
guard complete the rule, and without a clone arm no SUPPORTED state is
emitted.

\paragraph{Known-truth simulation calibration.}
Binary tensors are drawn from specified $M\times T$ probability matrices: iid equal ability
at constant $q$ and with shared task difficulty; global dominance with a
clean dominant fixed member, the reviewer's $0.5/0.9$ configuration, and
heterogeneous dominance; and crossover alternatives of increasing
strength over two latent task types, plus difficulty-related partial
crossover. The grid spans $M\in\{3,9,20\}$, $T\in\{100,400,1000\}$,
$R\in\{3,5,10,20\}$, complete data, 5\% MCAR, and 3\% hardness-related
missingness; the paper table focuses on the $(9,400,\cdot)$ acquisition
design with 300 Monte Carlo datasets per cell (150 in sweeps). Every
cell reports the support, refutation, abstention and error rates of four
procedures (plug-in; one-split frozen gain; repeat-cross-fitted gain;
clone-calibrated v3 decision), interval coverage against the true
parameter, and mean estimates. The plug-in
frequently false-supports under the tested nulls; v3's worst observed
null support rate is $2\%$. Its power reaches $100\%$ for the strong
two-specialist alternative at $R=10,20$, while the smaller cyclic
crossovers remain largely undetected even at $R=20$. These empirical
rates characterize the tested grid, not a general size or power guarantee. The earlier
eight mechanism-derived classes and the four repeat-based classes
(C9--C12, with C9/C10 now paired with registered same-code clone arms)
continue to match their predefined expected states; invariant tests
additionally assert permutation invariance,
dominance$\neq$complementarity, identity refusal, and the two review
counterexamples directly.

\subsection{Finite-repeat bias and clone-null calibration}
Writing $\hat{q}_h(x)$ for a repeat-averaged estimate,
$\mathbb{E}\max_h \hat{q}_h(x) \geq \max_h \mathbb{E}\hat{q}_h(x)$
by Jensen for each task, with strict inequality when finite-$R$ noise can
change the per-task argmax. The plug-in \emph{headroom} is a difference of
two such maxima; Jensen bounds each term separately and therefore does NOT
establish that the difference is upward biased in general --- in a
two-member, two-task crossing example the plug-in can be downward biased
(true $H{=}0.40$, expected plug-in over three repeats $\approx0.34$). What
is established is stronger and narrower: under same-code exchangeability
the true $H_{\rm stable}$ is zero while the plug-in is positive with
probability approaching one (selection noise on the per-task argmax;
$\widehat H{=}8.69$\,\% in the $q{=}0.9$, $M{=}9$, $T{=}400$, $R{=}3$
known-truth simulation, and $\WpVthreeClonePlugin$\,\% descriptively on
the sealed clone arm's $383$ complete tasks; the real panel gives
$\WpVthreePlugin$\,\% on its $350$ complete tasks).
They use the same complete-task sets as each arm's $G$, but are not
the paired comparison used for $D$. The calibrated procedure never decides from the plug-in:
a discovery/validation cross-fit freezes the per-task member mapping on
discovery repeats and scores it on held-out repeats against the best
\emph{fixed} member selected on the discovery repeats. Finite discovery
data need not identify a globally dominant member. The real-minus-clone
difference uses the clone arm as an observed sampling reference; it is
not an analytic removal of all finite-$(R,T)$ selection bias. Inference
uses a two-arm Monte Carlo randomization reference ($+1$ correction; exact at the equal-ability
boundary, empirically checked across the dominance grid rather than
asserted analytically throughout the cone; Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}).
The identity gates handle the
degenerate case outright by requiring provenance-verified independent
executions; identical matrices without distinct provenance are refused.

\subsection{Validation on known-truth controls} The frozen
package has two layers: the original eight mechanism-derived classes
$\times$ 2 instances (16 controls, expectations unchanged), plus four
repeat-based classes $\times$ 2 instances exercising the stability paths
single-shot controls cannot reach --- global dominance (C-rank
SUPPORTED, C-comp REFUTED), equal-mean crossover skill (C-rank
INSUFFICIENT, C-comp SUPPORTED under clone calibration, and the visible
stratum makes D/E SUPPORTED), a condition-mixing trap, and a
cloned-repeats trap (both must abstain); C9/C10 carry registered
same-code clone arms as required by the v3 decision. Expected states
are fixed before any blinded run and derive
from the generating mechanisms. Invariant tests additionally assert
permutation invariance, dominance$\neq$complementarity, identity
refusal, the two review counterexamples (iid noise and
$0.5/0.9$ dominance must not support; crossover must support with
adequate repeats), and that the decision never supports without a clone
arm. Calibration matched on all state cells; the blinded set (12
controls + 2 auditor challenges re-executed from the pre-committed,
fixed specification under the current code) matched on all $14\times 6$
cells: zero false supports, the positive control recognized, and the
abstention cap holds. These discrete controls complement, but do not
replace, the Monte Carlo false-support/power calibration of
\S\ref{sec:power}, which reports the v3 test's empirical false-support rates and power on the tested grid. The earlier
stored challenge result whose CH2 numbers contradict its own spec
(best-fixed 0.94 is unreachable under the generating rule, which forces
0.79) is retained as provenance only; spec-faithful re-execution matches
the auditor's pre-committed expectations on every cell.
