\begin{abstract}
Automatically generated LLM harnesses can differ in source code, executed
mechanisms, and correctness outcomes. These differences need not imply
reproducible additional capability. We examine the distinction on BIRD
text-to-SQL. A discovery population had 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on
all evaluated outcomes despite distinct source code; trace inspection found
absent mechanisms, untriggered branches, and broken multi-turn paths.
A subsequent factorial study crossed strategy forcing and an admission gate
over six builders and three seeds, using 1{,}169 questions and the official
BIRD scorer. Free generation already yielded 8.6\,pp observed oracle
headroom. Post-review reanalysis of the archived experiment estimates the
combined intervention at $\RevisionPrimaryDelta$\,pp (95\% CI
$\RevisionPrimaryCI$), with no clear improvement. A one-seed MATH-500
probe provides a parallel cross-domain diagnostic: it has 10.6\% outcome
disagreement and 4.75\,pp observed headroom, while bare remains the best
fixed member. The implemented gate also excludes two allowed prompt-level
strategy types, so the BIRD contrast cannot isolate verification alone.
Repeated executions show outcome instability, which a single-run oracle can
exploit without stable mechanism complementarity. We distinguish observed headroom from stable task--harness
advantages and identify the controls needed to connect either to usable
selection gains. The current evidence establishes these measurement limits;
it does not yet identify stable complementarity beyond repeated sampling.
\end{abstract}
