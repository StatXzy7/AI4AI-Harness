\subsection{The Population Covers More Tasks Than Any Single Member}
\label{sec:f2-headroom}
The generated population covers more tasks than its best fixed member.
Across $\RcvPairedN$ analysis tasks, the union of $35$ admitted
generated members and \bare{} reaches $\RevisionMathOracle\%$ oracle
coverage, compared with $\RevisionMathBest\%$ for \bare{}.
Every generated member is individually worse
($\MathGenMin$--$\MathGenMax\%$). All $\RcvSingleCells$ of
$\RcvSingleCells$ single-execution cells are observed, yielding
$\RevisionMathHeadroom$ pp of additional coverage available to an
oracle after execution.

This coverage comes with substantial variation in scored outcomes:
$\MathUnique$ of the $36$ correctness vectors are unique, mean
pairwise disagreement is $\RevisionMathDisagreement\%$
(maximum $\MathMaxDisagreement\%$), and the union repairs
$\MathUnionRepair\%$ of baseline errors. The next question is how
much of this variation survives repetition. We evaluate a selected
program panel against same-code controls on the same $\RcvPairedN$
tasks, with a fresh baseline mean of $\RcvBaselineAcc\%$.
The repeat-based $D$ measures the panel's held-out selection gain
relative to clones; the full-population coverage and panel estimates
refer to their respective populations and executions
(Appendix~\ref{app:crossdomain}).
