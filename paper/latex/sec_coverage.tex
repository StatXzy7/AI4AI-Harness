\subsection{Single-Run Coverage in Generated Populations}
\label{sec:f2-headroom}
We first ask whether the generated population supplies correct
answers beyond those of its best fixed member. All
$35$ admitted generated members are evaluated once on $400$ tasks.
Their union with \bare{} reaches $98.50\%$ oracle coverage, versus
$93.75\%$ for the best fixed member, \bare{}. Yet every generated
member is individually worse ($68.25$--$91.50\%$).
The resulting $4.75$\,\% headroom is an opportunity available in
hindsight, not an observed routing gain.

The outcomes are diverse: $33$ of $36$ correctness vectors are unique,
mean pairwise disagreement is $10.6\%$ (maximum $33.0\%$), and the
union repairs $76.0\%$ of baseline errors. None of these quantities
says how to choose a member before execution. This single-run study
has no clone control of its own; two reasoning-heavy builders also
exhausted generation budgets on most slots, limiting representation.
This raises the next question: can additional executions of identical
code also yield a positive oracle estimate? The repeat study introduces
that control on a selected subset.
Its $D$ does not correct the full population's $4.75$\,\% headroom,
and its fresh baseline mean $\WpBareAcc\%$ is a separate estimate
(Appendix~\ref{app:crossdomain}).
