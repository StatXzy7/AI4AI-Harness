\subsection{Single-Run Coverage in Generated Populations}
\label{sec:f2-headroom}
We first ask whether the generated population supplies correct answers
beyond its best fixed member. All $35$ admitted generated members and
\bare{} have one scored execution on each of the same $\RcvPairedN$
analysis tasks: $\RcvSingleCells$ of $\RcvSingleCells$ cells are observed.
Their union reaches $\RevisionMathOracle\%$ oracle coverage, versus
$\RevisionMathBest\%$ for the best fixed member, \bare{}. Every generated
member is individually worse ($\MathGenMin$--$\MathGenMax\%$).
The resulting $\RevisionMathHeadroom$ pp headroom quantifies
additional correct answers available to an oracle after execution.

The outcomes are diverse: $\MathUnique$ of $36$ correctness vectors are
unique, mean pairwise disagreement is $\RevisionMathDisagreement\%$
(maximum $\MathMaxDisagreement\%$), and the union repairs
$\MathUnionRepair\%$ of baseline errors. These quantities describe
the population's available correct answers. The repeat study evaluates
a selected program panel against same-code controls on the same
$\RcvPairedN$ tasks. Its $D$ measures the panel's held-out selection
gain relative to clones, and its fresh baseline mean is
$\RcvBaselineAcc\%$. The full-population headroom and panel estimates
thus describe their respective populations and executions
(Appendix~\ref{app:crossdomain}).
