AI-generated harness populations can be exactly what their source files suggest
and nothing like what routing needs. We measured the syntactic--behavioral gap on a
genuinely distinct-source population, diagnosed its mechanisms at the trace level, and
showed the missing headroom exists on the task. A pre-registered factorial study over
six builders and three seeds, on 1{,}169 questions from databases never opened during
development under the official BIRD scorer, then delivered a two-fold answer: the
combined strategy-forced, conformance-gated intervention shows \textbf{no clear
improvement} ($-0.16$\,pp, CI $[-0.90, +0.82]$), and the decomposition shows
\textbf{why} --- the conformance gate alone \emph{reduces} headroom ($-0.67$\,pp, CI
$[-1.17, -0.13]$), strategy forcing alone is null, and the two substitute rather than
complement. Mechanism fidelity and population complementarity trade against each other,
and clean-protocol free generation already produces headroom-rich populations at these
builder strengths.

The practical output stands: measure disagreement, repair, and harm before routing
over a generated population --- and do not assume that verifying what a harness claims
to do will make the population more useful.

	extbf{Limitations.} One domain family (text-to-SQL). Phase-I results come from a
discovery design with disclosed task overlap and a proxy judge. The routing boundary
covers the tested predictors and splits only; cache-off and repeat-run audits (R2/R3)
are pending.
