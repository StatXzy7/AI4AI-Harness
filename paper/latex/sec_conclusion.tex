On the tested MATH panel, more programs provide no gain for the frozen
selector, and more executions of the baseline match the portfolio's
$\RcvReplayFullCoverage\%$ oracle coverage at $27$ executions.
The contribution of this study is to explain what the intervening
signals do establish: identical programs can yield positive headroom,
and distinct programs can exhibit strongly repeatable score patterns
dominated by losses. Coverage, repeatability, and usable specialization
therefore answer different questions about a harness population.

The same-code reference, repeated-execution analysis, and frozen
selection evaluation make these questions separately testable.
Supporting BIRD traces connect program descriptions to implementation,
activation, and output validity. Together, the findings motivate a
concrete objective for harness design: produce task advantages that
survive fresh executions and can be converted into better decisions.
To justify additional programs through specialization, those decisions
must improve on spending the relevant inference budget on further
executions of a fixed program.
