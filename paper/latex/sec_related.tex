\paragraph{Automated agent and harness design.}
ADAS treats agent programs as a search space \citep{adas2024}.
TTHE, Self-Harness, JIT-Agent, and Task-CoEvolve generate or adapt
scaffolding around a frozen model
\citep{tthe2026,selfharness2026,jitagent2026,coevolve2026}.
HarnessLens, HarnessFix, AHE, HarnessBank, the Harness Handbook, and
HarnessForge examine behavior through rollout allocation, traces,
prediction checks, descriptors, localization, or joint policy evolution
\citep{harnesslens2026,harnessfix2026,ahe2026,gatedqd2026,handbook2026,harnessforge2026}.
Low behavioral diversity has also been reported for automatically
designed agents \citep{metaagents2025}, with conceptual precedents in
program equivalence and mutation testing \citep{budd1982,papadakis2019}.
Our question concerns attribution in the resulting population:
source differences may not change execution, while outcome differences
may occur even with identical source. The trace study and clone
control examine these two directions separately.

\paragraph{Repeated sampling and ensembles.}
Pass@$k$, self-consistency, and identical-agent ensembles exploit extra
attempts \citep{chen2021codex,selfconsistency2022,moreagents2024};
even nominally temperature-$0$ calls can vary \citep{nondeterminism2023}.
We do not claim that sampling gains are new. We use them as an explicit
reference when evaluating generated-program complementarity.
Post-execution verifiers \citep{llmverifier2026} and ensemble selection
\citep{caruana2004} also have different information from a policy that
chooses before any answer is available. Our oracle replay and frozen
selector retain this distinction.

\paragraph{Selection and resource allocation.}
Instance-dependent harness quality \citep{nouniversal2026,nonmonotone2026}
and HELIX outcome matrices \citep{helix2026} motivate going beyond the
best fixed member. STS, GRASP, counterfactual routing, and RouteLLM
select among scaffolds, skills, search actions, or models
\citep{sts2026,grasp2026,cfrouting2026,routellm2024}, extending questions
studied in algorithm selection and portfolios
\citep{rice1976,xu2008satzilla,kotthoff2016}.
The distinction between quantitative and qualitative interaction
\citep{gailsimon1985} explains why varying effect sizes need not change
the best action. We connect task-wise crossovers to a same-code
reference and held-out repeats, then evaluate selection separately.
This yields a diagnostic contribution, not a new routing algorithm.
