\textbf{What the studies show.}
Population utility is a stable task--harness interaction after
execution noise is separated: an oracle asks who was correct after
execution, whereas a router must commit beforehand. On BIRD, source-code
diversity is uninformative about behavioral difference ($\rho=-0.01$,
$21/66$ identical vectors despite passing code-difference checks), with
three named execution routes showing where distinct programs fail to
realize a distinct strategy. On the exploratory MATH-500 population,
one-run oracle coverage is $4.75$\,pp above the best fixed member, which
is \bare{} itself. On the pre-registered panel, byte-identical programs
reproduce a positive repeat-averaged plugin ($2.67$\,pp on the clone
arm), while the calibrated difference is $D=\WpVthreeD$\,pp (CI
$\WpVthreeDCI$, $p=\WpVthreeP$) and common-execution coverage is tied:
at three repeats the apparent gain is indistinguishable from extra
executions of one fixed program, and the predeclared rule abstains
rather than calling this equivalence; the frozen selector captures none
of the gap a pre-fixed random member loses ($\WpRandomDiff$\,pp).

\textbf{A reporting protocol.}
The results motivate four low-cost practices: (i) include a same-code
clone arm so additional executions are a measured baseline; (ii) collect
held-out independent repeats with discovery/validation splitting, and
report the repeat count and per-arm missingness; (iii) report one-run
coverage opportunity, repeat-stable interaction, and pre-execution
selection value as three distinct numbers with distinct information
sets; (iv) report slots, executions, and tokens separately, running any
budget-matched comparison at the claimed unit.

\textbf{Scope.}
The BIRD evidence covers one SQL target, six builders, and three seeds
(W1/W3 analyses descriptive); the MATH panel is a low-call-filtered draw
on previously seen tasks, not an unseen-task test. At $R=3$ the decision
is powered only for strong crossovers ($0\%$ power for an
$H=17.5$\,pp interaction) and the coverage gate independently failed on
$12.5\%$ of real-arm tasks, so the abstention is a measurement boundary,
not evidence of absence --- more repeats are the direct route to
resolving it. The panel spent $3.73\times$ the clone arm's tokens, so
the equal-execution replay (not realized cost) is the cost-controlled
comparison, and calibration is an empirical tested-DGP result over $100$
cells, not an analytic dominance-cone guarantee. The portable claim is
the measurement distinction and its controls across two domains; other
panels, targets, and repeat counts are the open extension.
