\paragraph{What additional coverage tells us about program diversity.}
Our results separate three properties often expected to coincide in a
program population: finding additional correct answers, retaining task
advantages across executions, and making those advantages useful for
selection. The same-code control makes this distinction concrete.
Byte-identical programs produce positive oracle headroom even after
averaging repeats, although their stable complementarity is zero by
construction. Additional coverage is therefore a real sampling
opportunity, but attributing it to program specialization requires
further evidence. The control changes the interpretation of a population's
success: the relevant comparison includes what repeated execution of a
fixed program can already provide.

\paragraph{Repeatability must be interpreted through the direction of advantage.}
The generated panel adds a second distinction. Its strong residual
score-pattern correlation shows that program identity is associated with
persistent differences, yet those differences predominantly identify
where generated members lose to the baseline. This is consistent with
the crossover requirement in Eq.~\ref{eq:identity}: variation in the
size of a disadvantage can be highly repeatable without changing which
program should be chosen. A specialization claim consequently needs
evidence about who wins on which tasks, as well as whether those wins
recur. In this panel, the asymmetry between persistent losses and wins
explains why repeatable structure offers little observed support for
switching away from the baseline. The frozen classifier's direct choice
of \bare{} on every task is consistent with this pattern; its zero gain
does not establish a limit on all possible selectors.

\paragraph{Designing mechanisms around the tasks that need them.}
The BIRD traces give this statistical distinction an execution-level
counterpart. An advertised operation must be implemented, activated on
the relevant input, and produce a valid answer before it can contribute
to performance. The inactive repair branch is particularly instructive:
SQL can execute successfully while answering the question incorrectly,
so an execution-error trigger can leave precisely that failure untouched.
This motivates designing a mechanism together with the task condition
that should activate it. A useful next experiment would compare the same
program with a mechanism enabled and disabled on tasks that exercise its
intended trigger, then test whether any advantage survives fresh
executions. Such interventions would examine a causal contribution;
the present traces locate failures along this path.

\paragraph{Evaluating the value of a larger program population.}
The findings suggest that evaluation should follow the decision the
population is meant to improve. For pre-execution routing, the target is
a task advantage predictable from information available before the
answer is known. For portfolios that execute several programs, the
target is the gain delivered by an implementable answer-selection or
aggregation procedure. Our oracle replay measures available answers
and leaves this latter decision open. Its full-budget tie shows why
additional fixed-program executions are an essential comparator.
Future studies should combine this comparator with repeat counts
calibrated to detect task-wise crossovers and comparisons at matched
model-call or token budgets. This would test whether adding programs
changes the value of inference beyond the sampling opportunities it
creates.

\paragraph{Scope and unresolved questions.}
The MATH evidence concerns a selected low-call panel, one solver, and
three repeats. The repeat diagnostic and common-complete-set reanalysis
were developed after collection; recovery preserved the programs,
tasks, solver, and repeat count. The $\RcvExcludedN$ excluded tasks
are harder than the $\RcvPairedN$ included tasks
(Appendix~\ref{app:wp1r}), and recorded score patterns include
answer-format effects. The calibrated low power at three repeats leaves
stable complementarity unresolved: \textsc{Insufficient Evidence}
does not imply equivalence. Selection results concern the frozen policy,
and replay matches harness executions, with model calls and tokens
varying by program. BIRD uses separate populations and scoring
conditions, so its traces supply supporting execution evidence rather
than a causal explanation of the MATH outcomes. Broader solver and
program populations remain to be tested.
