\textbf{Fidelity--complementarity trade-off.} The confirmatory decomposition is the
study's central lesson: mechanism-conformance filtering costs headroom
($-0.67$\,pp, CI excluding zero). A harness that executes exactly what it declares can
still be behaviorally redundant, while a sloppier candidate can repair items the clean
ones miss --- and cannot harm, from the selector's perspective, because a router can
always decline it. Verifying \emph{what a harness says it does} and increasing
\emph{what a population is worth} are different objectives, and here they conflict.

\textbf{Reliability and quality are different axes.} The yield decomposition shows the
gate rejecting $\sim$30\% of neutral-valid candidates; the headroom decomposition shows
those rejections were net-negative for the population. Generation reliability (can the
builder emit a valid artifact) and population quality (does the admitted set carry
complementarity) must be reported separately --- collapsing them hides which one failed.
