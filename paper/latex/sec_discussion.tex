\paragraph{Implications for evaluation.}
A positive oracle gain answers whether additional correct answers
were available. The same-code control shows why it cannot also answer
whether different programs supplied different task abilities.
Evaluations should report coverage, repeat-stable task advantages,
and realized policy gains as separate results, with a repeated
fixed-program reference for resource comparisons. If a gain is
attributed to a mechanism, traces must establish what ran and an
intervention must test its contribution. This reporting separates
useful sampling from specialization without discounting either.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The controlled MATH panel is a low-call draw under one target on
previously evaluated tasks; its v3 analysis was revised after
collection. Missingness exceeds the declared gate, and simulations
show that three repeats can miss substantial crossovers. The
nonpositive calibrated mean therefore does not establish absence
or equivalence. Zero routing gain concerns one selector; the replay
matches executions, not tokens. BIRD retains separate single-run
protocols, judging, and caching conditions. Its execution failures
cannot be used as a causal explanation for MATH.
