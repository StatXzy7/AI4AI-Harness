\textbf{The central insight.} Population utility is a stable
task--harness interaction after execution noise is separated, not a
diversity statistic: a one-run oracle asks who happened to be correct; a
router must pick the advantageous member beforehand.

\textbf{What the evidence establishes.} Source, mechanism, and one-run
outcome diversity are distinct measurements. The Phase-I pattern did not
recur in Phase II, though protocol changes prevent attribution; the
corrected gate reanalysis shows no clear headroom improvement and does
not identify verification alone; the diagnostic of
\S\ref{sec:diagnostics} separates what the numbers license --- a stable
ranking difference is not complementarity, abstention is not refutation.

\textbf{What remains unidentified.} Same-code reruns can disagree and
create oracle gains; flip rates measure instability, not the standard
error of a treatment contrast. Independent repeat validation against an
equal-budget clone population and cross-mechanism calibration
(Appendix~\ref{app:gatecontrols}) are needed before attributing headroom
to stable additional capability; the fresh MATH-500 collection
(\S\ref{sec:wp1r}) supplies the first such test on one panel.

\textbf{Limitations.} One text-to-SQL target, six fixed builders, three
generation seeds; the reanalysis adds no independent executions and W1/W3
remain descriptive; the MATH-500 one-run probe has no repaired-gate
control, with clone/repeat/selector controls from the separate fresh
collection. Residual-error coverage remains a stated design hypothesis,
not a tested win.
