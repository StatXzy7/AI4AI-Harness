009                                              A BSTRACT
010
011            Automatically generated LLM harnesses can differ in source code, executed
012            mechanisms, and correctness outcomes without providing reproducible additional
013            capability. We study this distinction on BIRD text-to-SQL. A discovery popula-
014            tion had 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on all evaluated outcomes despite distinct
015            source code; trace inspection found absent, untriggered, and broken mechanisms.
016
               A subsequent factorial study crossed strategy forcing and an admission gate over
               six builders and three seeds, using 1,169 questions and the official BIRD scorer.
017
               Free generation yielded 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. Post-review reanal-
018
               ysis estimates the combined intervention at −0.16 pp (95% CI [−0.83, +0.87]),
019            with no clear improvement. A one-seed MATH-500 probe provides parallel cross-
020            domain evidence: 10.6% outcome disagreement and 4.75 pp headroom, while bare
021            remains best. The gate excludes two allowed prompt-level strategy types, so the
022            BIRD contrast cannot isolate verification. Repeats show that one-run oracles can
023            exploit execution instability. We therefore frame population utility as stable, pre-
024            dictable task–harness interaction, with residual-error coverage as a corresponding
025            selection principle. The present data motivate but do not test that principle as
026            a positive intervention, and establish why observed headroom is insufficient evi-
027
               dence of deployable population value.

029   1   I NTRODUCTION
030
031   An LLM harness is the program of prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps a frozen model.
032   Automatically generating many such programs creates an evaluation problem: what evidence shows
033   that a new program provides a new capability? Different source code may execute the same path;
034   different paths may produce the same answer; different answers on one run may reflect execution
035   randomness rather than a repeatable advantage.
036   We study this problem on BIRD text-to-SQL (Li et al., 2023). In a Phase-I discovery population, 21
037   of 66 generated-candidate pairs had identical correctness vectors. Including the shipped react har-
038   ness and bare baseline gives 36 identical pairs out of 91. Pairwise code similarity had no observed
039   rank association with outcome disagreement (ρ = −0.01). Trace inspection found three distinct
040   situations: claimed mechanisms absent from control flow, mechanisms present but untriggered on
041   the inspected tasks, and broken multi-turn implementations. These observations motivate an audit
042   of the measurement process; they do not establish that generated harnesses generally collapse.
043   A subsequent pre-specified factorial study crossed strategy forcing and conformance gating over six
044   builders and three seeds. Its primary evaluation used 1,169 questions from nine databases outside
045   protocol development, with one disclosed 18-item smoke-test exception. Under this revised setting,
046   free ungated generation already produced 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. The Phase-I pattern
047   therefore did not recur, but changes in generation, judge, data, and execution prevent attribution to
048   any one protocol component without a controlled bridge.
049   The original gate also changes the admissible strategy space: two allowed single-call prompt trans-
050   formations cannot pass its mechanism check. Consequently, the intervention combines mechanism
051   screening with verification. We preserve that experiment and report a versioned post-review reanal-
052   ysis rather than treating it as a repaired-gate trial. The primary contrast remains −0.16 pp (95%
053   CI [−0.83, +0.87]). This unresolved estimate neither demonstrates improvement nor establishes
      equivalence.

      Our contribution at this stage is an empirical account of three measurement boundaries:
055
056         1. Code, trace, and outcome diversity differ. Discovery examples show why an inactive
057            branch and a missing mechanism require different tests.
058         2. Admission and inference define the comparison. A factorial study and an explicit cor-
059            rection of its data and statistical chain show which intervention was actually evaluated and
060            what its estimate supports.
061         3. Observed oracle value is not stable or usable value. Repeat instability and ex-
062
               ploratory selection results motivate separate reporting of oracle accuracy, best-fixed ac-
               curacy, repeated-run advantages, and realized selector performance.
063
064   The decisive remaining question is whether a real harness population outperforms an equal-budget
065   population of independent executions of the same code, with advantages that survive independent
066   repeats. The current study has not completed that control. We therefore treat stable complementarity
067   as an estimand to be tested, not a discovered property.
068
      This boundary suggests a sharper object for population construction: useful routing depends on
069
      stable task–harness interaction after execution noise is separated. A corresponding design hypothesis
070   is to value a candidate by its residual error coverage over the current population, rather than by
071   standalone accuracy; the present study motivates but does not test this principle.

      2.1   H ARNESS GENERATION FOR LLM S
076
077   A growing line of work generates task-specific scaffolding around a frozen model. TTHE evolves
078   text-to-SQL harnesses with an LLM proposer/judge loop (Nie et al., 2026); Self-Harness (Zhang
079   et al., 2026b) shows a model can improve its own harness; JIT-Agent (Zhang et al., 2026a) trains
080   a model to generate a harness per task at inference time; Task-CoEvolve (Miyai et al., 2026) co-
081   evolves tasks and scaffolds. A concurrent 2026 cluster makes harness evolution behavior-aware
082   from the verification side: HarnessLens (Xu et al., 2026) allocates rollout budget using behavior-
083   level checks during evolution; HarnessFix (Chen et al., 2026) repairs harnesses from trace-grounded
084   diagnoses; AHE (Lin et al., 2026) verifies each proposed edit against self-declared behavioral pre-
085
      dictions; gated semantic quality-diversity search (Luo et al., 2026) evolves populations over LLM-
      assigned pathology descriptors; Harness Handbook (Wang et al., 2026) attacks behavior localization
086
      and outer-loop search.
087
088   Our work is complementary but distinct in unit of analysis and question. Table 1 contrasts the two
089   nearest systems. HarnessLens uses behavior as a signal inside an evolution loop to allocate compute;
090   we ask whether the resulting population is behaviorally diverse as an outcome property, and how
091
      it changes under generation-side interventions. HarnessBank (Luo et al., 2026) documents search-
      space collapse and maintains a semantic diversity library with gated screening; our conformance
092
      gate does not score semantics at all — it verifies mechanically, via counterfactual trace probes, that
093
      a declared mechanism executes — and our design compares admission policies through a factorial
094   contrast. Because the implemented gate also excludes allowed prompt transformations, the current
095   experiment does not isolate verification’s causal contribution.

124   2.3   B EHAVIORAL DIVERSITY IN LLM POPULATIONS
125
126   Diversity of LLM outputs has mostly been studied at the sample level (self-consistency, diverse
127   decoding) or the agent-policy level. The observation that syntactically distinct programs can exhibit
128   identical behavior echoes coverage gaps documented in program synthesis and mutation-testing
129
      practice; our contribution is to instantiate and quantify it for AI-generated harness populations, with
      a mechanism-level taxonomy.

      3.1   H ARNESSES , OUTCOMES , AND THE THREE METRICS
141   A harness H wraps a frozen target model M : it receives the natural-language task x, issues one or
142   more calls to M , optionally executes intermediate artifacts (e.g. SQL), and returns a final answer
143   scored programmatically. With H0 the bare single-call baseline and Y ∈ {0, 1} correctness, the
144   harness marginal value is ∆(x, H, M ) = Y (x, H, M ) − Y (x, H0 , M ) ∈ {−1, 0, +1} (+1 repair,
145   −1 harness-induced harm).
146
      We propose that harness-generation and harness-routing pipelines report three precondition metrics
147   on a held-out set, all computable from the outcome matrix any evaluation already produces: (i)
148   pairwise outcome disagreement — over all harness pairs, the fraction of tasks where correctness
149   differs; (ii) union repair rate — the fraction of bare errors some population member repairs; (iii)
150   bare-inclusive oracle headroom

      3.3   T RACE TAXONOMY: WHY CODE DIVERSITY FAILS TO BECOME BEHAVIORAL DIVERSITY
196
197   Auditing execution traces (the sequence of LLM calls and artifact executions per task) decomposes
198   “different code” into three failure classes:
199
200          • T1 — mechanism described, not executed (6/12 candidates). The harness issues ex-
201            actly one LLM call and never executes an artifact — the identical execution path as bare.
202            Docstrings claim retrieval, self-checking, or token caps; none exists in the control flow.
203          • T2 — mechanism present but untriggered (react, 10/10 audited tasks). The first SQL
204
               executes successfully, so error repair is not triggered and the final SQL equals bare’s. This
               does not show that repair feedback lacks a causal path when a failure occurs.
205
             • T3 — broken multi-turn path (1/12). The one candidate implementing genuine multi-
206
               turn repair returns prose as final SQL, converting bare-correct items into harness-wrong.
207
208   The taxonomy is actionable: T1 demands an execution-path acceptance gate, T2 demands controlled
209   faults to test trigger and feedback use, T3 demands executable-output validation — all generation-
210   protocol requirements.

475   6     D ISCUSSION AND C ONCLUSION
476
477   The central insight. Population utility is an interaction problem, not a diversity statistic: a useful
478   member must retain a stable, task-conditional relative advantage after execution noise is separated.
479   A one-run oracle asks whether some member happened to be correct; a router must identify the
480   advantageous member before observing the answer. Code diversity, mechanism fidelity, and one-
481   run disagreement are therefore insufficient proxies.
482   What the evidence establishes. Source diversity, executed mechanisms, and one-run correctness
483   differences are distinct measurements. The Phase-I pattern did not recur in the revised Phase-II
484   setting; multiple protocol changes prevent identifying the cause. The corrected analysis of the orig-
485   inal gate shows no clear headroom improvement. Because that gate also excludes allowed prompt
      strategies, it does not identify the effect of verification alone.


                                                                   9
      Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027




486
      What remains unidentified. Same-code reruns can disagree and create oracle gains. Outcome flip
487   rates measure instability, not the standard error of a treatment contrast, and cannot define a numer-
488   ical noise floor for that contrast. A matched-cost clone control, independent repeat validation, and
489   validated calibration across mechanism types (Appendix G) are needed before attributing headroom
490   to stable additional capability.
491
      The post-review provider acquisition attempts did not yield a complete common pool: transport in-
492
      terruptions left completed responses, unclosed requests, and unstarted attempts in separate auditable
493   states. These records are retained for provenance and missingness accounting, but are excluded
494   from claims about population performance, cost, or stable complementarity. Thus, the manuscript
495   does not use provider availability or partial acquisition as evidence for the unresolved clone-control
496   question.
497
      Limitations. The main comparison uses one text-to-SQL target and six fixed builders, with three
498
      generation seeds per builder. Phase I is discovery evidence with disclosed task overlap, a proxy
499   judge, and a post-trace human control. The new statistical analysis is post-review and does not
500   add independent executions. K-matched and R2 results now share the corrected data and resam-
501   pling chain. W1/W3 now share the canonical data but remain descriptive; selection uncertainty and
502   independent utility validation are outstanding. The one-seed MATH-500 free-arm probe provides
503   parallel cross-domain support: outcome diversity and one-run headroom reappear, but bare remains
504   the best fixed member. Its protocol is in Appendix H; it supplies no clone, repeat, repaired-gate, or
505   stable-complementarity test. The practical implication is a falsifiable selection rule: value a candi-
506   date by its residual error coverage over the current population. The present archive does not yet test
507
      that rule against top-accuracy, random, or diversity-based selection on held-out tasks, so we state it
      as a design hypothesis rather than an empirical win.
508
509   Generated code, observed outcome diversity, and reproducible additional capability should be eval-
510   uated separately. Our archived experiments expose failures at the first two levels and leave the third
511   unresolved. Reporting absolute oracle accuracy, best-fixed accuracy, admission policy, execution
512   cost, and repeat variability makes that boundary inspectable. Establishing practical value requires
513
      independently validated selection gains beyond same-code repeated sampling.
