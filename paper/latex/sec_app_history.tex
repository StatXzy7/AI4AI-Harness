\subsection{Early generation-protocol probes and their limits}\label{sec:phase1probes}
Phase I also probed generation-side fixes --- a strategy-forced free-form protocol
(assigned mechanism, import/smoke acceptance) and a compiled Harness IR. Under the
strategy-forced protocol two of three builders produced populations whose point
estimates exceeded the pre-specified thresholds (Qwen3.8-Flash 5.96,
DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 7.95\,\%; the old-protocol arm missed at
4.64\,\%), and per-strategy acceptance ordered with builder identity
(GLM-5.3-Flash 3/8, Qwen3.8-Flash 7/8, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 8/8,
under the logged tool). These
observations motivate Phase II but are not confirmatory, for three disclosed reasons:
the design was two nested contrasts that cannot separate protocol from builder main
effects; the early runs lack per-attempt logs, so budget equality cannot be audited;
and acceptance was an import/smoke check, not the counterfactual conformance gate of
\S\ref{sec:f2-admission}. Full condition details are in Appendix~\ref{app:phase1-provenance}.

\paragraph{Generation-null failures (M0).} Re-running the original optimize loop
with Qwen3.8-Flash and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp builders produced 40 candidates byte-identical to the
\bare{} baseline source. Code identity alone does not imply identical stochastic
executions. This run supplies no distinct-code population; it is reported as a distinct,
more primitive failure mode --- the pipeline produced no distinct population at all.
The collapse claims above rest exclusively on the genuinely distinct-source discovery
population.

\paragraph{Figure data.} Figure~\ref{fig:phenomenon} compares source
similarity with correctness disagreement for the observed harness pairs.

\paragraph{Generation-provenance gaps.} The early free-form populations
were produced in-session; their generation tool was not preserved, and
per-attempt logs do not exist for them --- so budget equality cannot be
audited for those runs, and we do not claim it. Their acceptance was an
import/smoke check at evaluation time, \emph{not} the Phase-II
counterfactual conformance gate. The subsequent generation procedure
records every attempt (3 retries,
16{,}384-token budget, per-attempt stage and finish reason); the completed
per-strategy grids and the builder-acceptance ordering (3/8, 7/8, 8/8)
come from this logged tool and are post-hoc with respect to the frozen
admission decisions.

\subsection{Upstream code-extraction audit}\label{app:extraction}
Across the 36 forced C/D cells, all 108 \texttt{format\_guard} attempts
failed neutral validity: 71 logs report unterminated Python strings,
36 report no Python fence, and one reports a provider timeout.
The archived generator's non-greedy Python-fence extractor terminates at
the first subsequent triple backtick, including one inside a Python
string. Three synthetic, syntactically valid programs containing SQL fences
in an ordinary string, docstring, or regex become invalid prefixes;
the extractor nevertheless sets its truncation flag to false.
The audited extractor matches the retained implementation, but the
historical attempt records do not verify which version actually ran. Full builder messages
were not saved by this generator. Thus the failure pattern is compatible
with extraction damage, not proof that all historical failures had this
cause or that repaired candidates would pass admission.
We found 74 retained extracted programs, all syntactically invalid; three
coexist with no-fence logs and are retained as provenance conflicts,
not attributed to those attempts. These findings place a generation
interface failure alongside the gate's explicit strategy exclusions.

\subsection{Post-review provider acquisition status}
The post-review provider acquisition attempts did not yield a complete common pool: transport interruptions left completed responses, unclosed requests, and unstarted attempts in separate auditable states. These records are retained for provenance and missingness accounting, but are excluded from claims about population performance, cost, or stable complementarity. Thus, the manuscript does not use provider availability or partial acquisition as evidence for the unresolved clone-control question.


\subsection{Historical BIRD selection analyses}\label{app:rich}
This selection analysis and the fingerprints in
Appendix~\ref{app:bird-fingerprints} are post-hoc on the archived confirmatory cells (18
builder$\times$seed cells, 384 admitted candidates, core-400 outcomes).
Both use the same record-combination rules, fixed tasks, and member
identity checks as the main reanalysis. They remain descriptive: these tasks had already been
analyzed, and this is not independent transfer validation.

\paragraph{Diversity-aware selection.} Each cell pools all admitted
candidates across arms. The reanalysis retains the legacy seed, 100
random database-half splits, 200 random subset draws per split and cell,
and candidate-selection rules. It records a new deterministic schedule:
the historical set-derived cell order was not archived, so identical
historical splits and draws cannot be recovered from the seed alone.
Selection uses development databases; evaluation uses the complementary
databases. The overlapping splits are not 1{,}800 independent experiments.
Methods are top-$k$ development accuracy, uniform random subsets, greedy
development oracle coverage, and greedy mean accuracy plus
$\lambda$ times oracle coverage for $\lambda\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$.
All candidate choices are frozen within a split before its evaluation.

Table~\ref{tab:selection} retains the common AD single-call in every evaluated
subset; $k$ counts candidates only. Candidate-only values are recorded separately
to distinguish the historical estimand. Oracle and best-fixed use
held-out outcomes as descriptive upper bounds. Dev-fixed instead chooses
one member using development accuracy, with ascending member-name ties
and single-call first. Deterministic methods already retain a globally dev-best
candidate; differences in this comparator may arise from which tied
maxima survive, not improved maximum development accuracy.

\paragraph{Re-selection does not establish interval coverage.}
An exact counterexample rules out a universal 95\% coverage guarantee for
our proposed positive-weight re-selection percentile interval. Let nine
independent databases each contain one Bernoulli($p$) task state. Sixteen
fixed candidates comprise eight state-0 specialists followed by eight
state-1 specialists; single-call is always wrong. Apply the canonical $k=8$
rules to four development and five held-out databases. With the recorded
tie rules, the expected div-only minus top-acc headroom statistic is
$(1-p^4)\,\mathbb{E}[\min(B,5-B)/5]>0$, where $B\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(5,p)$.
On the event that all nine states coincide, every positive reweighting
produces zero headroom for both methods, so the percentile interval is
$[0,0]$. Coverage is therefore at most $1-p^9-(1-p)^9$, or 61.26\% at
$p=0.1$. Averaging identical cells or overlapping fixed splits does not
remove this event. This bound concerns the stated construction and
interval, not BIRD-specific coverage or the impossibility of other
inference methods.

\subsection{Routing and exploratory selection}\label{sec:boundary}
The Phase-I leave-one-harness-out predictor did not beat the best fixed
harness in the tested DeepSeek population ($-3.4$\,\%); the Qwen estimate
was $+0.9$\,\% (CI $[-5.2,+6.9]$), and task-held-out repair AUROC was
0.512. These predictor and split-specific results do not establish value
prediction. The canonical W1 analysis is likewise descriptive: at $k=8$,
diversity versus top-accuracy selection changes oracle by
$\RevisionWoneOracle$\,\%, best-fixed by $\RevisionWoneBest$\,\%, and
headroom by $\RevisionWoneHeadroom$\,\%; the development-selected fixed
member changes by $\RevisionWoneFixed$\,\%. None is a validated routing
gain (Appendix~\ref{app:rich}).

\subsection{Historical archive diagnostics and rescoring}\label{app:archive-analysis} Applied to the MATH-500 archive
(36 members $\times$ 400 tasks) and the 18-cell BIRD Phase-II archive. On
BIRD, the integrity class (A) is REFUTED\_WITHIN\_SCOPE for every cell: the
combined observations contain overlapping acquisition records that
duplicate on the canonical key (resolved by the archived first-write-wins
policy, with recorded verdict-conflict counts), so the coverage numbers
below are computed on that deduplicated view, not on a conflict-free
archive; judge replay is not executed on this path and is reported as
not-run, not passed. Subject to that scope, all cells show single-shot
coverage (B SUPPORTED); stability, selectability, and deployability are
INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE by the frozen rules because the archives contain no
matched repeats and no dev/eval split for policy training --- not because
costs are absent: per-record logical calls are archived and are read on
the same canonical deduplicated view as the rest of the reanalysis
($\RevisionLogicalCalls$ calls across $\RevisionCostRows$
candidate--task core-subset records, Table~\ref{tab:cost}), though
token/dollar costs remain unrecoverable. On MATH the frozen selection policy, trained on the
first 80 of the 400 eval tasks held out of all D/E claims (the archive
contains no dev-set executions: the 100-task dev split of
Appendix~\ref{app:crossdomain} was used only for generation smoke tests,
so the policy split is carved from the eval side, leaving 320 scored
tasks), ties with dev-fixed (single-call) on every eval task, so
selectability and deployability are INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE rather than
refuted. Three published numbers were corrected (membership-basis of the
outcome-vector count, now 33/36 members including single-call and 32/35 generated
harnesses; the generated-harness accuracy range 0.6825--0.915, previously
printed as 0.68--0.87; the headroom decomposition with best-fixed = single-call
stated explicitly), two overloaded metric names were disambiguated
(union-all-fail vs any-candidate-fail), and one preserved-judgment control
confirms a reported pair (union repair 76.0\% / union-all-fail 0.8\%) under
the explicit definition. A re-execution of the versioned
endpoint-sensitive judge over all 14{,}400 MATH verdicts changes 14 of them
--- all v1 false rejects of interval-endpoint answers on one task
; the v1 replay itself is internally consistent, and an
earlier stored 0-flip artifact is not reproduced by the corrected analysis
and is superseded. The bracket-merging defect the new judge was designed to
catch therefore does fire in this archive, on exactly one task.

\subsection{Quarantined MATH prompt-protocol pilot} First, the clean-protocol
diversity result replicates in a second domain with a different output
space. Second, a quarantined pilot round supplies an exploratory comparison:
when the same six builders were (mistakenly) given a GSM8K-style task
description whose numeric answer format did not match MATH-500, the
generated population remained behaviorally diverse (16.2\% disagreement,
19 unique outcome vectors) while its union repair collapsed to 18\% --- and
code-level inspection of the catastrophic members reproduced the Phase-I
trace taxonomy verbatim (a generate-then-verify harness whose verifier
rejected nearly every answer and whose fallback returned the stale loop
variable or ``0''). Diversity without utility, induced by a prompt-side
protocol error invisible to code-level checks: the measurement mandate, not
any code-level audit, is what detects it.
