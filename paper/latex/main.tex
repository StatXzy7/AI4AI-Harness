\begin{abstract}
Automatically generated LLM harnesses offer a rich design space, but their
source-code, execution, and outcome diversity must be measured separately.
On BIRD text-to-SQL, trace inspection distinguishes absent, untriggered,
and broken mechanisms, and free generation yields 8.6\,pp of observed
oracle headroom across 1{,}169 questions; a one-seed MATH-500 probe
shows 10.6\% outcome disagreement and 4.75\,pp headroom. We formalize
one-run oracle gains, stable ranking, stable complementarity,
pre-execution selectability, and budgeted utility as five separately
reported claim classes, checked by ordering-invariance,
dominance-versus-complementarity, cloned-repeat, and condition-mixing
controls with hash-bound challenge re-execution; the procedure also
corrects one of our earlier verification claims. On the archived
factorial study the combined screening intervention estimates
$\RevisionPrimaryDelta$\,pp (95\% CI $\RevisionPrimaryCI$), supporting
the measurement contribution rather than intervention superiority. A
fresh pre-registered MATH-500 collection supplies the decisive
equal-budget control: an eight-harness panel and nine same-code clone
slots, each run for three independent repeats. The panel shows stable
member ranking, but stable complementarity is not established and its
discovery-selected advantage is indistinguishable from same-code
repeated execution. The framework turns apparent diversity into
auditable evidence and identifies the controls required before any
deployment claim.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Ethics Statement}
All experiments use public BIRD and MATH-500 benchmark data and generated
program artifacts; no human participants, private records, or personally
identifying information are collected.  Model calls are reported as part of
the experimental provenance, and the paper does not release credentials or
provider responses that would expose private data.  The main ethical risk is
overstating what one-run oracle gains imply for deployed routing; we therefore
report the unresolved selection and cost claims explicitly as
\textsc{Insufficient Evidence}.

\subsection*{Reproducibility Statement}
The supplementary source includes the frozen diagnostic implementation,
control manifests, and hash-bound result records.  `REPRODUCE.md` gives the
exact offline commands for unit tests, calibration, blinded controls, archive
diagnoses, and the LaTeX build.  The reported provider-dependent archives are
retained with their provenance and hashes; no claim requires access to a live
model API to rerun the diagnostic itself.
