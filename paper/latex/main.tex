\begin{abstract}
Automatically generated LLM harnesses offer a rich design space, but their
source-code, execution, and outcome diversity must be measured separately.
We define five separately reported claim classes --- measurement
sufficiency, single-shot coverage, \emph{stable ranking},
\emph{stable complementarity}, pre-execution selectability, and
budget-matched utility --- and give the two stability claims
size-controlled tests. The complementarity test freezes a per-task member
mapping on discovery repeats, scores it on held-out repeats against the
best \emph{fixed} member (global dominance can then never look like
complementarity), and calibrates against a same-code clone arm with a
Monte Carlo randomization test. Known-truth simulations establish its
false-support rate and power across member, task, and repeat counts:
the prior repeat-averaged estimator false-supports on pure execution
noise in every null regime we test, whereas the calibrated test keeps the
false-support rate at its nominal level and detects interaction only when
repeats suffice for a reliable mapping. Applied to a fresh pre-registered
MATH-500 collection (eight generated harnesses and nine byte-identical
\bare{} clone slots, three independent repeats each), the panel shows a
stable member ranking, yet the clone-calibrated selection gain is
$\WpVthreeD$\,pp (95\% CI $\WpVthreeDCI$; randomization $p=\WpVthreeP$):
the evidence neither establishes a gain beyond independent executions of
one fixed program at the declared $1$\,pp margin nor equivalence, and a
frozen coverage gate keeps the verdict at \textsc{Insufficient
Evidence}. On BIRD text-to-SQL, trace inspection and a versioned
factorial reanalysis ($\RevisionPrimaryDelta$\,pp, 95\% CI
$\RevisionPrimaryCI$) document what single-run numbers cannot establish.
The framework turns apparent diversity into auditable evidence and, by
construction, does not manufacture the complementarity it searches for.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Ethics Statement}
All experiments use public BIRD and MATH-500 benchmark data and generated
program artifacts; no human participants, private records, or personally
identifying information are collected.  Model calls are reported as part of
the experimental provenance, and the paper does not release credentials or
provider responses that would expose private data.  The main ethical risk is
overstating what one-run oracle gains imply for deployed routing; we therefore
report the unresolved selection and cost claims explicitly as
\textsc{Insufficient Evidence}.
