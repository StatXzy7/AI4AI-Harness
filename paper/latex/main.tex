\begin{abstract}
Automatically generated LLM harnesses can differ in source code, executed
mechanisms, and correctness outcomes without providing reproducible additional
capability. We study this distinction on BIRD text-to-SQL. A discovery
population had 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on all evaluated outcomes
despite distinct source code; trace inspection found absent, untriggered, and
broken mechanisms. A subsequent factorial study crossed strategy forcing and
an admission gate over six builders and three seeds, using 1{,}169 questions
and the official BIRD scorer. Free generation yielded 8.6\,pp observed oracle
headroom. Post-review reanalysis estimates the combined intervention at
$\RevisionPrimaryDelta$\,pp (95\% CI $\RevisionPrimaryCI$), with no clear
improvement. A one-seed MATH-500 probe provides parallel cross-domain evidence:
10.6\% outcome disagreement and 4.75\,pp headroom, while bare remains best.
The gate excludes two allowed prompt-level strategy types, so the BIRD contrast
cannot isolate verification. Repeats show that one-run oracles can exploit
execution instability. We therefore frame population utility as stable,
predictable task--harness interaction, and separate a stable ranking
difference from stable complementarity: by a two-action identity, a member
that dominates everywhere gives the former with zero of the latter. These
boundaries are packaged as an offline diagnostic over five claim classes,
whose implementation is checked against invariants (ordering invariance,
dominance $\neq$ complementarity, refusal of condition-mixed or cloned
repeats) and validated on mechanism-derived controls with hash-bound
challenge re-execution; applying it also corrects one of our own earlier
verification claims. The present data motivate but do not test
residual-error coverage as a positive intervention, and establish why
observed headroom
is insufficient evidence of deployable population value.
\end{abstract}
