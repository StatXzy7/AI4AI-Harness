\begin{abstract}
Should an inference budget buy more programs or more executions of
one program? We study this choice by separating answer coverage,
repeatable performance, and usable selection in generated LLM harnesses.
On $\RcvPairedN$ MATH-500 tasks, we compare eight generated programs
plus a baseline with nine byte-identical baseline slots, each executed
three times. Identical programs already yield $\RcvClonePlugin$ percentage
points of repeat-averaged oracle headroom. Generated programs exhibit
much stronger residual score-pattern correlation
($\RcvRealRho$ versus $\RcvCloneRho$), but this structure is dominated
by persistent losses across $\RcvLossTasks$ tasks; persistent wins occur
on one. The frozen selector gains $\RcvPolicyGain$ percentage points,
and both arms reach $\RcvReplayFullCoverage\%$ oracle coverage at
27 executions. Held-out repeats leave stable complementarity unresolved.
Supporting BIRD traces show how implementation, activation, and output
validity shape executed behavior. In the tested panel, repeatable differences primarily reveal
where programs lose, while additional baseline executions match
full-budget coverage.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Ethics Statement}
All experiments use public BIRD and MATH-500 benchmark data and generated
program artifacts; no human participants, private records, or personally
identifiable information are collected.  Model calls are reported as part of
the experimental provenance, and the paper does not release credentials or
provider responses that would expose private data. The results distinguish
recorded answer coverage from the performance of a selector operating
before execution.

\subsection*{Reproducibility Statement}
The manuscript specifies the populations, task splits, execution
conditions, scoring rules, statistical procedures, and sensitivity
analyses used for the reported results. The appendices provide
additional protocol details, calibration results, and worked examples.
The review materials present the methodological and empirical evidence
through these descriptions, tables, and examples.
