\begin{abstract}
Populations of automatically generated LLM harnesses underpin routing and
value prediction, which presuppose that population members \emph{disagree} on
outcomes. We audit this premise on BIRD text-to-SQL. A Phase-I discovery
study found a standard harness-evolution population syntactically diverse yet
behaviorally collapsed --- 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on every task,
code similarity unassociated with outcome disagreement --- and traced the failure
by execution-trace audit to mechanisms described but never executed. A
hand-written positive control showed the routing headroom exists (7.3\,pp);
the failure was on the generation side. A pre-specified confirmatory
factorial study (strategy forcing $	imes$ conformance gate; six builders,
three seeds; 1{,}169 questions from never-opened databases; official BIRD
scorer) then revised the story twice. First, collapse proved
\emph{protocol-sensitive}: under a clean equal-budget protocol, free ungated
generation already produced headroom-rich populations (8.6\,pp oracle
headroom, 31\% union repair). Second, the intervention showed \emph{no clear
improvement} ($-0.16$\,pp, CI $[-0.90, +0.82]$); the apparent negative gate
effect was substantially size-confounded ($-0.30$\,pp at matched $K$, CI
spanning zero) and the bare-outcome noise floor (14.5\% flip rate at
temperature 0) exceeds the effects in question. The stable conclusion is a
measurement mandate: report outcome disagreement, repair, harm, and headroom
before routing over generated populations --- and audit the generation
protocol, since the collapse we found was invisible to every code-level check
the pipeline had passed.
\end{abstract}
