An LLM harness is the program of prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps
a frozen model. Automatically generating many such programs creates an
evaluation problem: what evidence shows that a new program provides a new
capability? Different source code may execute the same path; different paths
may produce the same answer; different answers on one run may reflect
execution randomness rather than a repeatable advantage.

We study this problem on BIRD text-to-SQL \citep{bird2021}. In a Phase-I
discovery population, 21 of 66 generated-candidate pairs had identical
correctness vectors. Including the shipped \react{} harness and \bare{}
baseline gives 36 identical pairs out of 91. Pairwise code similarity had
no observed rank association with outcome disagreement ($\rho=-0.01$).
Trace inspection found three distinct situations: claimed mechanisms absent
from control flow, mechanisms present but untriggered on the inspected
tasks, and broken multi-turn implementations. These observations motivate
an audit of the measurement process; they do not establish that generated
harnesses generally collapse.

A subsequent pre-specified factorial study crossed strategy forcing and
conformance gating over six builders and three seeds. Its primary
evaluation used 1{,}169 questions from nine databases outside protocol
development, with one disclosed 18-item smoke-test exception. Under this
revised setting, free ungated generation already produced 8.6\,pp observed
oracle headroom. The Phase-I pattern therefore did not recur, but changes
in generation, judge, data, and execution prevent attribution to any one
protocol component without a controlled bridge.

The original gate also changes the admissible strategy space: two allowed
single-call prompt transformations cannot pass its mechanism check.
Consequently, the intervention combines mechanism screening with
verification. We preserve that experiment and report a versioned
post-review reanalysis rather than treating it as a repaired-gate trial.
The primary contrast remains $\RevisionPrimaryDelta$\,pp (95\% CI
$\RevisionPrimaryCI$). This unresolved estimate neither demonstrates
improvement nor establishes equivalence.

Our contribution at this stage is an empirical account of three measurement
boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
\tightlist
\item \textbf{Code, trace, and outcome diversity differ.} Discovery examples
show why an inactive branch and a missing mechanism require different tests.
\item \textbf{Admission and inference define the comparison.} A factorial
study and an explicit correction of its data and statistical chain show
which intervention was actually evaluated and what its estimate supports.
\item \textbf{Observed oracle value is not stable or usable value.} Repeat
instability and exploratory selection results motivate separate reporting
of oracle accuracy, best-fixed accuracy, repeated-run advantages, and
realized selector performance.
\end{enumerate}
The decisive remaining question is whether a real harness population
outperforms an equal-budget population of independent executions of the
same code, with advantages that survive independent repeats. The current
study has not completed that control. We therefore treat stable
complementarity as an estimand to be tested, not a discovered property.
