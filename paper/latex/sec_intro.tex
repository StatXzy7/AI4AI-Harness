An LLM harness is the program of prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps
a frozen model. When an automatically generated population of such programs
shows higher coverage against a best-fixed reference, two explanations are
possible: its members may implement distinct task specializations, or the
population may simply contain more independent executions of one fixed
stochastic program. The two are not the same claim. Two
identical programs that each solve a task with probability $0.5$
(independent errors) score $0.75$ coverage by running both once---a
$25$\,pp gain over running a single one, with zero
task-specialization evidence. A population oracle gap can
therefore be an observation about execution noise rather than
program diversity.

We study this question on BIRD text-to-SQL \citep{bird2021} and MATH-500
\citep{math500}, using three sources of evidence. First, in a BIRD
discovery population, a trace audit finds that 21 of 66 generated-candidate
pairs have identical correctness vectors (36 of 91 including
\react{} and \bare{}), even though pairwise code similarity has no
rank association with outcome disagreement ($\rho=-0.01$). Three
execution-level routes explain the collapse: mechanisms
claimed in code but absent from control flow (6 of 12
candidates), mechanisms present but never triggered (\react,
10/10), and broken multi-turn paths (1/12). Second, a
separate exploratory study on MATH-500 (35 generated
harnesses, 400 tasks, one run each) shows $4.75$\,pp
one-run oracle headroom over the best fixed member (which is the
\bare{} harness itself). Third, a fresh pre-registered
collection pairs eight generated harnesses (plus \bare{}) against nine
byte-identical
\bare{} clone slots, three independent repeats each: the
repeat-averaged descriptive plugin is positive on both
arms ($2.54$\,pp on the real panel's $350$ complete tasks,
$2.67$\,pp on the clone arm's $383$), yet the
cross-fitted frozen-selection gains match those of the same-code arm
($D=-0.10$\,pp, 95\% CI $[-1.55,1.46]$, randomization
$p=.7742$, on $342$ paired complete tasks), and the predeclared coverage gate is
unmet on $12.5\%$ of real-arm tasks. Independently, a
three-run audit of the single \bare{} harness on the $400$ BIRD core
tasks finds $14.5\%$ of its verdicts changing across repeats, and three
\bare{} executions treated as three members show a descriptive
$6.50$\,pp one-run gap with no corresponding program specialization.

These observations motivate evaluating apparent complementarity
as three separate quantities---source-code diversity, execution-level
diversity, and outcome diversity---rather than as a single scalar.
Our contributions are:

\textbf{1. An empirical separation of code-level, execution-level, and
outcome-level diversity in generated harness populations,} with a trace
taxonomy showing exactly where syntactically distinct programs
fail to realize distinct strategies.

\textbf{2. Clone-controlled, repeat-based evaluation.} A
pre-registered collection with an explicit cross-fitted
frozen-selection statistic and a same-code randomization reference
measures how much apparent complementarity is attributable to extra
executions, with the decision rule validated as instrumentation on
known-truth simulation grids.

\textbf{3. A reporting protocol for harness populations:} one-run
coverage opportunity, repeat-stable task interaction, and pre-execution
selection value reported as separate numbers at separately stated
budgets (slots, executions, tokens), with scope stated once per study.

The decisive question is whether distinct generated programs deliver a
frozen-selection gain over spending the same executions on one fixed
program, with advantages that survive independent repeats. On the fresh
pre-registered panel the calibrated difference is indistinguishable
from the clone arm's and the coverage gate is unmet, so the verdict is
\textsc{Insufficient Evidence} rather than either a gain or a
$1$\,pp equivalence: at three repeats, apparent complementarity is
consistent with execution sampling rather than a discovered property of
the panel. This reframes the target of population construction ---
useful routing depends on stable task--harness interaction after
execution noise is separated, the estimand the controlled study is built
to test.
