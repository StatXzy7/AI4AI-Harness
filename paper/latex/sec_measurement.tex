\subsection{Three metrics}\label{three-metrics}
If harness quality is conditional --- \(Q(H \mid x, M)\) rather than a
scalar \(Q(H)\) --- then a harness \emph{population} is valuable only
insofar as its members disagree on outcomes in a structured,
repair-bearing way: code-level difference is cheap to obtain, but
outcome-level difference is what routing consumes. We propose that any
harness-generation pipeline (or any harness-routing paper) report three
precondition metrics on a held-out set:
\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Pairwise outcome disagreement.} Over all harness pairs
  \((H_i, H_j)\), the fraction of tasks where
  \(Y(x,H_i,M) \neq Y(x,H_j,M)\). Zero disagreement means the population
  is behaviorally one harness.
\item
  \textbf{Union repair rate.} The fraction of bare errors --- tasks
  where \(Y(x,H_0,M)=0\) --- such that \emph{some} harness in the
  population answers correctly. This bounds what any selector over the
  population could achieve.
\item
  \textbf{Oracle headroom.} Accuracy of the per-item oracle selector
  \(\max_i Y(x,H_i,M)\) (taken over harnesses \emph{and} the bare
  baseline) minus the best fixed harness. Positive headroom means the
  population's value is \emph{conditional on the item} --- exactly the
  signal a value predictor or router would need.
\end{enumerate}
These three metrics are cheap (they require only the outcome matrix
\(Y \in \{0,1\}^{|X| \times |K|}\) that any evaluation already
produces), hard to game (they are defined on outcomes, not on code), and
diagnostic in combination: high disagreement with no union repair is
noise; union repair with no headroom means one harness dominates;
headroom without disagreement is impossible by definition.
\subsection{Trace audit: why code diversity fails to become
behavioral
diversity}\label{trace-audit-why-code-diversity-fails-to-become-behavioral-diversity}

Outcome metrics say \emph{that} a population collapsed; they do not say
\emph{why}. We audit execution traces --- the sequence of LLM calls and
artifact executions per task --- and find that ``different code''
decomposes into three failure classes:
\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{T1 --- Pure prompt variants (6/12 candidates).} The harness
  issues exactly one LLM call and never executes an artifact --- the
  identical execution path as \texttt{bare}. The docstring may claim
  retrieval, self-checking, or token caps; none of it exists in the
  control flow. The mechanism is \emph{described}, not
  \emph{implemented}.
\item
  \textbf{T2 --- Feedback that cannot change the answer (react, 10/10
  audited tasks).} The harness executes the SQL and feeds errors back to
  the model, yet its final SQL is byte-identical to \texttt{bare}'s on
  every audited task. Execution feedback exists in the loop but has no
  causal path to the output.
\item
  \textbf{T3 --- Buggy multi-turn paths (1/12).} The only candidate
  implementing genuine multi-turn repair returns prose as final SQL,
  converting bare-correct items into harness-wrong --- the single
  negative-value behavior in the population.
\end{itemize}
This taxonomy turns ``AI-generated harnesses are behaviorally
collapsed'' into an actionable diagnosis: T1 demands an execution-path
acceptance gate (Section~6), T2 demands feedback mechanisms with a verified
causal path, T3 demands executable-output validation --- all three are
\emph{generation-protocol} requirements, independent of the benchmark.
\subsection{The collapse, quantified}\label{the-collapse-quantified}
On the day-1 population (14 harnesses × 60 tasks, GLM-5.3-Flash target,
TTHE proposer protocol): average pairwise outcome disagreement
\textbf{3.5\%} (maximum 13.3\%; \textbf{28 of 78 pairs fully
identical}), union repair over bare errors \textbf{7.7\%} (2/26), oracle
headroom \textbf{3.3 pp} --- and \texttt{react} differs from
\texttt{bare} on \textbf{zero} tasks. Every candidate passed a
code-similarity check (``all files distinct, non-trivial diffs''): the
population is syntactically diverse and behaviorally one harness. To our
knowledge this is the first systematic measurement of this gap in
AI-generated harness populations.
