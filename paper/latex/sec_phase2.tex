The empirical studies use four evidence sources, presented in the order
of \S\ref{sec:diagnosis}: a BIRD text-to-SQL discovery population for the
code-to-execution trace audit (\S\ref{sec:f1-collapse}); a separate
exploratory MATH-500 one-run probe (\S\ref{sec:f2-headroom}); a fresh
pre-registered MATH-500 collection with matched repeats and a same-code
control (\S\ref{sec:f3-clones}); and a pre-specified BIRD admission-policy
experiment with a versioned corrected reanalysis
(\S\ref{sec:f2-admission}). Each study fixes its own
estimand, repeat count, missingness rule, and budget unit, and results
are never placed on a before/after line across studies.

\paragraph{Same-code clone control.}
The decisive design makes ``more independent executions of one fixed
program'' a measured arm rather than a hypothesis. The pre-registered
collection runs a panel of eight generated harnesses plus \bare{} and, in
parallel, nine equal-slot byte-identical \bare{} clone slots, for three
independent repeats each on the same $400$ MATH-500 tasks. Equal slots is
not equal executions and not equal tokens; all three budget units are
reported separately.

\paragraph{Cross-fitted frozen-selection statistic.}
\label{sec:calibration-test}
A repeat-averaged plugin $\widehat H$ maximizes noisy per-task estimates
and is retained only as a descriptive quantity. The scientific statistic
cross-fits a frozen selection: in each of three folds, two discovery
repeats freeze both the per-task member mapping $h^\star(x)=\arg\max_h
\bar q_h(x)$ and the single best fixed member $h_{\rm fix}$; the held-out
repeat scores the frozen mapping against that fixed member (never against
\bare{}). This gives $G_{\rm real}$ and $G_{\rm clone}$, and the decision
statistic is their paired difference $D=G_{\rm real}-G_{\rm clone}$ on
tasks complete in both arms, referenced against a two-arm within-task
Monte Carlo randomization distribution (member labels permuted
independently per task and repeat in both arms) with a paired task
bootstrap. The randomization reference is exact at the equal-ability
null where arm labels are exchangeable; over the rest of the dominance
cone finite-sample false support is checked empirically on known-truth
DGPs, not asserted analytically. Frozen safeguards include a
$\le10\%$ incomplete-task coverage gate in either arm, a $\ge30$
paired-task minimum, and no imputation of unknown cells (all-$0$/all-$1$
fill scenarios are reported instead). Without a clone arm the statistic
is descriptive and emits no supported complementarity.

\paragraph{Instrumentation validation on known truths.}
We validate the decision rule on simulated binary tensors with specified
member-by-task probabilities, spanning $M\in\{3,9,20\}$ members,
$T\in\{100,400,1000\}$ tasks, $R\in\{3,5,10,20\}$ repeats, complete
data, $5\%$ MCAR, and $3\%$ hardness-related missingness, with null
families (equal ability, including a heterogeneous-difficulty variant;
global dominance, including the $0.5/0.9$ configuration) and crossover
alternatives of increasing strength. The $100$ cells complete with zero
procedure errors. Table~\ref{tab:calibration} reports the
$M{=}9,T{=}400$ slice: the plugin false-supports at rate near one on
every null (including all-$q{=}0.9$ and the sealed clone arm), whereas
the clone-calibrated rule's worst observed null false-support is $2\%$
(equal ability, $M{=}9,T{=}100,R{=}3$; Wilson $[0.68,5.71]\%$). Power is
reported honestly with repeat count: a clean two-specialist crossover at
$H=17.5$\,pp is detected with $0\%$ power at $R=3$, $0.7\%$ at $R=5$,
and $100\%$ at $R=10,20$, while realistic $1$--$4$\,pp cyclic
interactions remain unresolved at $R=3$. These are tested-DGP
statements, not an analytic finite-sample size guarantee over the
dominance cone.

\paragraph{Missingness and task provenance.}
A task is \emph{complete} for an arm if it has a recorded verdict for
every member in every repeat; tasks with an unresolved remote call or a
carried unknown marker are excluded from that arm rather than imputed.
Point estimates are complete-case and exact on the included tasks; the
two arms have different complete-task sets, so arm-level descriptive
numbers are reported on their own sets and only $D$ is paired across the
intersection. The evaluation tasks had been executed in the earlier
one-run probe, so the collection is prospective in execution but
retrospective in tasks --- an independent-repeat study, not unseen-task
validation.
