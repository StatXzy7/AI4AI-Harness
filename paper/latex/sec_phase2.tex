\paragraph{Same-code slots isolate the sampling explanation.}
Running more members introduces more attempts as well as more code.
We therefore compare nine distinct members with nine byte-identical
\bare{} slots, each evaluated three times per task. The latter preserve
the opportunity for stochastic outcomes while removing program
specialization, providing an equal-ability reference.

\paragraph{Held-out repeats separate discovery from validation.}
\label{sec:calibration-test}
Choosing a task's best program and validating it on the same outcomes
would reuse lucky successes. Instead, each of three folds uses two
discovery repeats to freeze both a per-task member mapping and a
single best fixed member. The third repeat measures their accuracy
difference. Averaging folds gives $G_{\rm real}$ and $G_{\rm clone}$;
the fixed comparator is selected by discovery accuracy.
We then compare the arms on their common complete tasks:
\begin{equation}
D=G_{\rm real}-G_{\rm clone}.
\label{eq:clone-difference}
\end{equation}
Here the subtraction is of paired task-level gains. It asks whether
past outcomes identify choices that transfer to another execution
better for distinct programs than for identical ones. The diagnostic
measures cross-repeat transfer on the same evaluation tasks.

\paragraph{Complete observations and sensitivity govern interpretation.}
Every primary quantity, including both descriptive plugins and the two
cross-fitted gains, uses the same $\RcvPairedN$ complete tasks. Each arm
contains $\RcvCells/\RcvCells$ scored member--task--repeat cells
($100\%$ completeness); all estimates use recorded binary scores.
The original acquisition-coverage gate is assessed before subsetting
and is passed: $\RcvOriginalExcludedPct\%$ of tasks were incomplete,
below its $10\%$ ceiling. Positive-effect checks, including $D>1$ pp,
and the minimum of $30$ paired tasks remain unchanged. Known-truth
simulations assess repeat sensitivity. Appendix~\ref{app:wp1r} documents
selection into the analysis set; Appendix~\ref{app:statistics} gives the
rule, tie handling, uncertainty, and sensitivity analyses.
The acquisition protocol preceded collection; the v3 procedure and
common-complete-set analysis were determined afterward.
