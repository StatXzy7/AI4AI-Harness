An LLM \textbf{harness} $H$ is the program of prompts, tools, and
control flow wrapped around a frozen target model $M$: it receives a
natural-language task $x$, issues one or more calls to $M$, optionally
executes intermediate artifacts such as SQL, and returns a final answer
scored programmatically. Let $H_0$ denote the single-call \bare{}
baseline and $Y(h,x,r)\in\{0,1\}$ the correctness of member $h$ on task
$x$ on independent repeat $r$. The population $P$ we evaluate always
includes $H_0$.

\paragraph{Question~1: one-run coverage opportunity.}
Given a single execution matrix, the \emph{best-fixed accuracy} is
$\max_h \mathbb E_x Y(h,x,\cdot)$ and the \emph{one-run oracle
accuracy} is $\mathbb E_x \max_h Y(h,x,\cdot)$. Their difference is the
bare-inclusive oracle headroom
\begin{equation}
H(P)=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_{i}\max_{h\in P\cup\{H_0\}}Y(x_i,h)
-\max_{h\in P\cup\{H_0\}}\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_{i}Y(x_i,h).
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}
$H(P)$ records only item-dependent successes present in \emph{that}
execution matrix. It cannot establish a repeatable advantage or any
pre-execution decision, because it is computed with knowledge of who was
correct after the fact. Two simple indicators sit alongside it:
pairwise outcome disagreement (the fraction of tasks on which two
members differ) and the union repair rate (the fraction of \bare{}
errors some member corrects); disagreement without repair adds no
coverage, and repair that does not beat the best fixed member adds no
headroom.

\paragraph{Execution noise is large enough to make the distinction
material.} On the BIRD core-$400$ archive, a canonical replay finds
$2{,}391/28{,}000$ same-harness verdict flips against cached outcomes
($8.54\%$), and a dedicated three-run \bare{}-only audit finds $14.5\%$
of tasks changing \bare{} correctness at least once; three independent
\bare{} executions treated as three ``members'' show a descriptive
$6.50$\,pp one-run oracle gap even though they are one program. A
single-run $H(P)$ therefore counts both specialization and extra
sampling chances, motivating Question~2.

\paragraph{Question~2: repeat-stable task-conditioned advantage.}
For two members write $\Delta(x)=p_1(x)-p_0(x)$ for their per-task
success-probability difference and $(u)_+=\max(u,0)$. At the population
level,
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm stable}=\mathbb E\,\Delta(x)_+-(\mathbb E\Delta(x))_+
=\tfrac12\bigl(\mathbb E|\Delta(x)|-|\mathbb E\Delta(x)|\bigr),
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
so a member better \emph{everywhere} produces a stable ranking
difference with $H_{\rm stable}=0$: global dominance is not
complementarity, which requires the advantage to cross over tasks. This
is a two-member population identity and a conceptual anchor, not a
finite-sample bound: mappings and best-fixed members selected from a
finite number of noisy repeats carry selection noise, which the same-code
control measures in \S\ref{sec:f3-clones}. Stable ranking and stable
complementarity are consequently not a nested ladder.

\paragraph{Question~3: pre-execution selection value.}
An oracle reads who was correct after execution; a router must commit to
one member beforehand, a strictly smaller information set. The
practically deployable quantity is therefore the held-out gain of a
selector frozen before execution over the best fixed member --- not
post-execution oracle coverage. The remainder of the paper measures all
three questions separately and reports the budgets (slots, executions,
tokens) at which each is answered.
