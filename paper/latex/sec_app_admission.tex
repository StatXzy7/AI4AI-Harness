\subsection{Design and primary contrasts}
\label{sec:f2-admission}
A separate confirmatory BIRD study crosses six independent builders with
three seeds under strategy forcing (a named mechanism from a fixed set
versus free invention) and conformance gating, giving four arms:
II-A (free, ungated), II-B (free, gated), II-C (forced, ungated), and
II-D (forced, gated). The evaluation covers
$1{,}169$ questions from nine databases never opened during
development (one disclosed $18$-item smoke-test exception), with the
official BIRD execution scorer and a pre-outcome-frozen analysis later
re-run as a versioned corrected reanalysis. The implemented gate bundles
nontrivial-mechanism structural screening with conformance verification
--- two of eight forced prompt-transformation slots cannot pass even
when neutrally valid --- so the treatment does not isolate verification
causally.

establish equivalence. Structural admission is therefore not evidence
that a mechanism activates on relevant tasks or adds stable utility.

On the shared core-$400$ tasks the gate contrast is
$\RevisionGateDelta$\,\%, and the strategy and interaction secondaries
are likewise inconclusive (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}); the fixed-$K$
mean contrast $\RevisionKmatchedDelta$\,\% provides a population-size
sensitivity check. The open-mechanism arm ran only on
the development side and has no confirmatory estimand. These are all
single-run contrasts, and the execution variability reported in
the R2/R3 audits in Appendix~\ref{app:r2} ($8.54\%$ R2 verdict flips; $14.5\%$ of tasks
changing \bare{} correctness across three runs; a $6.50$\,\% same-code
one-run gap) characterizes them as coverage opportunities rather than
capability differences.
