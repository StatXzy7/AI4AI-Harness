\section{Same-Code Controls and Repeated-Execution Protocol}\label{app:wp1r}
All current MATH primary estimates use one $\RcvPairedN$-task,
fully observed analysis set. This section records how it was obtained
and documents score completeness, provenance, and sample selection.

\paragraph{Study objects, capture, and inclusion rule.}
The program panel, solver, judge, development split, and three repeat
identities remain fixed. A salted-hash draw selects one low-call member
per builder and two at-large seats, then adds \bare{}; the comparator
has nine identical baseline slots. The score snapshot captured on
September 26, 2026 at 05:15 UTC combines generated-arm ledgers through
continuation 10 and clone-arm ledgers through continuation 20. This
snapshot fixes the analysis inputs. We retain exactly the tasks with a scored
outcome for every required member and repeat in both arms and every
member in the single-run study, accepting either recorded binary
score at each required cell. The resulting $\RcvPairedN$ task identities
are frozen for every primary comparison. This inclusion rule is applied
after outcome inspection and is disclosed as a reanalysis.

\paragraph{Complete analysis set and excluded observations.}
The single-run matrix contains $36\times\RcvPairedN=\RcvSingleCells$
observed cells. Each repeat arm contains
$9\times3\times\RcvPairedN=\RcvCells$ observed cells. All three matrices
therefore have $100\%$ scored-observation completeness on the analysis
set. The same identities are used for policy accuracy and oracle replay.
Score completeness and acquisition accounting are recorded separately.

The fixed inclusion rule excludes $\RcvExcludedN$ problems with
$\RcvOriginalMissingCells$ unscored generated-arm cells; the clone
records on these excluded problems are complete. These problems are
excluded from the present analysis, and their unknown scores remain
unknown in the retained source records. The original excluded-task fraction is
$\RcvOriginalExcludedPct\%$, below the frozen $10\%$ gate, assessed
on the pre-subsetting acquisition records. By builder, unscored
cells are DeepSeek 5, ERNIE 4, GLM 9, Kimi 30, MiniMax 9, and Qwen 4.
The baseline is complete. Source records and incurred acquisition costs
remain preserved, including those outside the analysis set.

\paragraph{Identity, precedence, and conflicts.}
Continuation manifests are checked on 13 condition fields, including
solver, cache, judge, protocol, repeats, program identities, task/split
hashes, panel draw, and schedule. The original snapshot hashes are
verified and the new score matrices and task lists are hash-bound.
For each member--task--repeat identity, a completed score replaces an
unknown; the first completed score takes precedence when later records
differ. Five generated-arm cell identities have conflicting scores,
three of which lie on three included tasks; clones have none. Collector
and concurrency changes remain recorded as operational deviations.

\paragraph{Current estimates and sensitivity.}
The common-set plugins are $\RcvRealPlugin$\,pp (generated) and
$\RcvClonePlugin$\,pp (clone). The paired diagnostic is
$D=\RcvD$\,pp, with 95\% interval $\RcvDCI$\,pp and
\textsc{Insufficient Evidence}. A conflict-exclusion sensitivity removes
the three affected included tasks from both arms and recomputes all
choices on the remaining $\RcvConflictN$ fully observed tasks:
$D=\RcvConflictD$\,pp, interval $\RcvConflictDCI$\,pp, with the same
state. This explicitly labeled sensitivity subset is separate from the
$\RcvPairedN$-task primary analysis. Both estimates are computed
directly from recorded binary scores. Versioned artifacts preserve
earlier estimates and acquisition snapshots.

\subsection{Exploratory Repeatability and Score Sensitivity}\label{app:repeatability}
For each arm, let $A_r(h,x)=Y(h,x,r)-Y(h_0,x,r)$ for non-reference
members, with $h_0=$\bare{} or clone-c1. On the common complete tasks,
subtract the row and column means and add the grand mean separately
within each repeat. For each held-out repeat, correlate the flattened
residual matrix with the residual of the other two repeats' mean;
report the mean of the three correlations. A paired task bootstrap
with $2{,}000$ draws, resampling the same tasks in both arms, supplies
intervals. Generated correlation is $\RcvRealRho$ ($\RcvRealRhoCI$),
clone correlation $\RcvCloneRho$ ($\RcvCloneRhoCI$), and their
difference $\RcvRhoDiff$ ($\RcvRhoDiffCI$). This post-collection
diagnostic measures residual score-pattern repeatability after removing
additive effects.

The recorded-score asymmetry is substantial: generated members lose
to \bare{} in all three repeats on $\RcvLossPairs$ member--task pairs spanning
$\RcvLossTasks$ distinct tasks. All-three-repeat wins comprise eight pairs but
only one task, shared by all eight generated programs. These counts
summarize the observed three-repeat score patterns.
In that single task, the gold answer is \texttt{12}; all baseline
outputs conclude \texttt{\#\#\#\# 12th grade}, whereas a generated
member returns \texttt{12th grade}. The fixed extractor preserves
the complete \texttt{\#\#\#\#} payload and scores the former zero,
but falls back to numeric extraction and scores the latter one.
The score difference arises from the extractor's format-dependent
branches. We retain the recorded scores and interpret this example
as a format-sensitive repeated repair.
