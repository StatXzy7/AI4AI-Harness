\subsection{Harnesses and harness value}\label{harnesses-and-harness-value}

Following the harness-abstraction of TTHE, a \textbf{harness} \(H\) is a
program that wraps a frozen target model \(M\): it receives the
natural-language task input \(x\), issues one or more calls to \(M\),
optionally executes intermediate artifacts (e.g., SQL against the
database), and returns a final answer. Execution accuracy is scored
programmatically. For a task \(x\), we define the \textbf{harness
marginal value}

\[\Delta(x, H, M) \;=\; Y(x, H, M) - Y(x, H_0, M) \in \{-1, 0, +1\},\]

where \(H_0\) is the bare single-call baseline and \(Y \in \{0,1\}\) is
programmatic correctness; \(+1\) is a \emph{repair} (raw wrong, harness
correct), \(-1\) is \emph{harness-induced harm}. The deployment question
that motivates this paper --- predict \(\Delta\) before executing \(H\)
--- presupposes that \(\Delta\) is not almost-always zero. Our first
contribution is to take this presupposition seriously and measure it.

\subsection{Domain, target, and data splits}\label{domain-target-and-data-splits}

We instantiate the problem on \textbf{BIRD text-to-SQL} (SQLite), whose
answers are judged by execution against gold query results --- no
model-based grading. The target model is frozen throughout:
\textbf{GLM-5.3-Flash} served through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint
(temperature 0); builders differ only in how harness \emph{code} is
produced. We use two databases, \texttt{card\_games} and
\texttt{formula\_1}: the \textbf{builder-visible set}
\(D_{\mathrm{build}}\) contains 8 \texttt{card\_games} questions, and
the \textbf{evaluation set} \(D_{\mathrm{eval}}\) contains \textbf{151
questions} (121 \texttt{card\_games}, 30 \texttt{formula\_1}), held out
from all builders (\(D_{\mathrm{build}} \cap D_{\mathrm{eval}} =
\emptyset\) by construction). We disclose two overlaps explicitly:
(i) 24 of the 151 items were also used in day-1 protocol-development
measurements (their day-1 outcomes informed the diagnosis of
Section~4, not any generation input); (ii) the four human control
harnesses (Section~5) were designed after inspecting day-1 traces on
these tasks, so the control's headroom is an existence proof rather
than a blind estimate. Harness populations are generated, frozen, and
hashed \emph{before} any evaluation on \(D_{\mathrm{eval}}\); no
harness is re-tuned after evaluation.

\subsection{Populations under comparison}\label{populations-under-comparison}

Each AI-generated population contains \(K=6\) harnesses produced under a
fixed budget and acceptance protocol. We compare seed baselines
(\texttt{bare} and the shipped \texttt{react}), the old TTHE proposer
protocol (condition A), strategy-forced free-form populations with GLM
(condition B; the audited logged re-run B3, K=3), Qwen (C), and DeepSeek
(D) builders, a compiled Harness-IR arm, and a four-harness human
positive control; Appendix~\ref{app:populations} details each condition,
including the selection rule for A, the unaudited 0/6 original B
observation, and the condition-by-condition acceptance records. Every
harness is accepted only after passing a smoke check (it runs and emits
executable SQL); the free-form arm additionally passes the
execution-path gate of Section~6.

\subsection{Design structure and pre-registered decision criteria}\label{design-structure-and-pre-registered-decision-criteria}

\textbf{The design is two nested contrasts, not a full factorial}, and
our claims are limited accordingly: the protocol contrast at a fixed
builder exists only for GLM (A vs B), the builder contrast exists only
under the gated protocol (B vs C vs D), and the IR arm has a protocol
axis but no builder axis; Section~6 interprets each contrast within its
own fixed factor and does not claim to separate main effects
(Appendix~\ref{app:design} details the design). Before running any
151-item evaluation, admission thresholds were frozen in the project
decision log: a population must reach \textbf{union repair rate over
bare errors $\geq$ 15\%}, \textbf{oracle headroom (vs.~best fixed harness)
$\geq$ 5 pp}, and \textbf{$\geq$ 3 harnesses with non-empty, pairwise-distinct
repair sets}; the audit trail --- the 20\% threshold in the v6 entry
(2026-08-31), set to 15\% in the v7 entry (2026-09-01), both before any
151-item outcome existed --- is disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.
Populations failing the thresholds are not subjected to downstream
value-prediction experiments. Admission is decided on point estimates by
the frozen rule; bootstrap intervals are reported alongside for
calibration, and Section~6 marks populations whose intervals include
sub-threshold values.
