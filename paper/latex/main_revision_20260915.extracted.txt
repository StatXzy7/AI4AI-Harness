011                                              A BSTRACT
012
013            Automatically generated LLM harnesses can differ in source code, executed
014            mechanisms, and correctness outcomes. These differences need not imply re-
015
               producible additional capability. We examine the distinction on BIRD text-to-
               SQL. A discovery population had 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on all evalu-
016
               ated outcomes despite distinct source code; trace inspection found absent mech-
017
               anisms, untriggered branches, and broken multi-turn paths. A subsequent facto-
018            rial study crossed strategy forcing and an admission gate over six builders and
019            three seeds, using 1,169 questions and the official BIRD scorer. Free genera-
020            tion already yielded 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. Post-review reanalysis of
021            the archived experiment estimates the combined intervention at −0.16 pp (95%
022            CI [−0.83, +0.87]), with no clear improvement. A one-seed MATH-500 probe
023            provides a parallel cross-domain diagnostic: it has 10.6% outcome disagreement
024            and 4.75 pp observed headroom, while bare remains the best fixed member. The
025            implemented gate also excludes two allowed prompt-level strategy types, so the
026
               BIRD contrast cannot isolate verification alone. Repeated executions show out-
               come instability, which a single-run oracle can exploit without stable mechanism
027
               complementarity. We distinguish observed headroom from stable task–harness
028
               advantages and identify the controls needed to connect either to usable selection
029            gains. The current evidence establishes these measurement limits; it does not yet
030            identify stable complementarity beyond repeated sampling.

033   1   I NTRODUCTION
034
035   An LLM harness is the program of prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps a frozen model.
036   Automatically generating many such programs creates an evaluation problem: what evidence shows
037   that a new program provides a new capability? Different source code may execute the same path;
038
      different paths may produce the same answer; different answers on one run may reflect execution
      randomness rather than a repeatable advantage.
039
040   We study this problem on BIRD text-to-SQL (Li et al., 2023). In a Phase-I discovery population, 21
041   of 66 generated-candidate pairs had identical correctness vectors. Including the shipped react har-
042   ness and bare baseline gives 36 identical pairs out of 91. Pairwise code similarity had no observed
043   rank association with outcome disagreement (ρ = −0.01). Trace inspection found three distinct
044
      situations: claimed mechanisms absent from control flow, mechanisms present but untriggered on
      the inspected tasks, and broken multi-turn implementations. These observations motivate an audit
045
      of the measurement process; they do not establish that generated harnesses generally collapse.
046
047   A subsequent pre-specified factorial study crossed strategy forcing and conformance gating over six
048   builders and three seeds. Its primary evaluation used 1,169 questions from nine databases outside
049   protocol development, with one disclosed 18-item smoke-test exception. Under this revised setting,
050
      free ungated generation already produced 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. The Phase-I pattern
      therefore did not recur, but changes in generation, judge, data, and execution prevent attribution to
051
      any one protocol component without a controlled bridge.
052
053   The original gate also changes the admissible strategy space: two allowed single-call prompt trans-
      formations cannot pass its mechanism check. Consequently, the intervention combines mechanism

      screening with verification. We preserve that experiment and report a versioned post-review reanal-
055   ysis rather than treating it as a repaired-gate trial. The primary contrast remains −0.16 pp (95%
056   CI [−0.83, +0.87]). This unresolved estimate neither demonstrates improvement nor establishes
057   equivalence.
058
      Our contribution at this stage is an empirical account of three measurement boundaries:
059
060         1. Code, trace, and outcome diversity differ. Discovery examples show why an inactive
061            branch and a missing mechanism require different tests.
062         2. Admission and inference define the comparison. A factorial study and an explicit cor-
063            rection of its data and statistical chain show which intervention was actually evaluated and
064            what its estimate supports.
065         3. Observed oracle value is not stable or usable value. Repeat instability and ex-
066            ploratory selection results motivate separate reporting of oracle accuracy, best-fixed ac-
067            curacy, repeated-run advantages, and realized selector performance.
068
      The decisive remaining question is whether a real harness population outperforms an equal-budget
069
      population of independent executions of the same code, with advantages that survive independent
070   repeats. The current study has not completed that control. We therefore treat stable complementarity
071   as an estimand to be tested, not a discovered property.

      2.1   H ARNESS GENERATION FOR LLM S
076
077   A growing line of work generates task-specific scaffolding around a frozen model. TTHE evolves
078   text-to-SQL harnesses with an LLM proposer/judge loop (Nie et al., 2026); Self-Harness (Zhang
079   et al., 2026b) shows a model can improve its own harness; JIT-Agent (Zhang et al., 2026a) trains
080   a model to generate a harness per task at inference time; Task-CoEvolve (Miyai et al., 2026) co-
081   evolves tasks and scaffolds. A concurrent 2026 cluster makes harness evolution behavior-aware
082   from the verification side: HarnessLens (Xu et al., 2026) allocates rollout budget using behavior-
083   level checks during evolution; HarnessFix (Chen et al., 2026) repairs harnesses from trace-grounded
084   diagnoses; AHE (Lin et al., 2026) verifies each proposed edit against self-declared behavioral pre-
085
      dictions; gated semantic quality-diversity search (Luo et al., 2026) evolves populations over LLM-
      assigned pathology descriptors; Harness Handbook (Wang et al., 2026) attacks behavior localization
086
      and outer-loop search.
087
088   Our work is complementary but distinct in unit of analysis and question. Table 1 contrasts the two
089   nearest systems. HarnessLens uses behavior as a signal inside an evolution loop to allocate compute;
090   we ask whether the resulting population is behaviorally diverse as an outcome property, and how
091
      it changes under generation-side interventions. HarnessBank (Luo et al., 2026) documents search-
      space collapse and maintains a semantic diversity library with gated screening; our conformance
092
      gate does not score semantics at all — it verifies mechanically, via counterfactual trace probes, that
093
      a declared mechanism executes — and our design compares admission policies through a factorial
094   contrast. Because the implemented gate also excludes allowed prompt transformations, the current
095   experiment does not isolate verification’s causal contribution.

124   2.3   B EHAVIORAL DIVERSITY IN LLM POPULATIONS
125
126   Diversity of LLM outputs has mostly been studied at the sample level (self-consistency, diverse
127   decoding) or the agent-policy level. The observation that syntactically distinct programs can exhibit
128   identical behavior echoes coverage gaps documented in program synthesis and mutation-testing
129
      practice; our contribution is to instantiate and quantify it for AI-generated harness populations, with
      a mechanism-level taxonomy.

      3.1   H ARNESSES , OUTCOMES , AND THE THREE METRICS
141   A harness H wraps a frozen target model M : it receives the natural-language task x, issues one or
142   more calls to M , optionally executes intermediate artifacts (e.g. SQL), and returns a final answer
143   scored programmatically. With H0 the bare single-call baseline and Y ∈ {0, 1} correctness, the
144   harness marginal value is ∆(x, H, M ) = Y (x, H, M ) − Y (x, H0 , M ) ∈ {−1, 0, +1} (+1 repair,
145   −1 harness-induced harm).
146
      We propose that harness-generation and harness-routing pipelines report three precondition metrics
147   on a held-out set, all computable from the outcome matrix any evaluation already produces: (i)
148   pairwise outcome disagreement — over all harness pairs, the fraction of tasks where correctness
149   differs; (ii) union repair rate — the fraction of bare errors some population member repairs; (iii)
150   bare-inclusive oracle headroom

      3.3   T RACE TAXONOMY: WHY CODE DIVERSITY FAILS TO BECOME BEHAVIORAL DIVERSITY
196
197   Auditing execution traces (the sequence of LLM calls and artifact executions per task) decomposes
198   “different code” into three failure classes:
199
200          • T1 — mechanism described, not executed (6/12 candidates). The harness issues ex-
201            actly one LLM call and never executes an artifact — the identical execution path as bare.
202            Docstrings claim retrieval, self-checking, or token caps; none exists in the control flow.
203          • T2 — mechanism present but untriggered (react, 10/10 audited tasks). The first SQL
204
               executes successfully, so error repair is not triggered and the final SQL equals bare’s. This
               does not show that repair feedback lacks a causal path when a failure occurs.
205
             • T3 — broken multi-turn path (1/12). The one candidate implementing genuine multi-
206
               turn repair returns prose as final SQL, converting bare-correct items into harness-wrong.
207
208   The taxonomy is actionable: T1 demands an execution-path acceptance gate, T2 demands controlled
209   faults to test trigger and feedback use, T3 demands executable-output validation — all generation-
210   protocol requirements.

475   6     D ISCUSSION AND C ONCLUSION
476
477   What the evidence establishes. Source diversity, executed mechanisms, and one-run correctness
478   differences are distinct measurements. The Phase-I pattern did not recur in the revised Phase-II
479   setting; multiple protocol changes prevent identifying the cause. The corrected analysis of the orig-
480   inal gate shows no clear headroom improvement. Because that gate also excludes allowed prompt
481   strategies, it does not identify the effect of verification alone.
482   What remains unidentified. Same-code reruns can disagree and create oracle gains. Outcome flip
483   rates measure instability, not the standard error of a treatment contrast, and cannot define a numer-
484   ical noise floor for that contrast. A matched-cost clone control, independent repeat validation, and
485   validated calibration across mechanism types (Appendix G) are needed before attributing headroom
      to stable additional capability.

      The post-review provider acquisition attempts did not yield a complete common pool: transport in-

      Limitations. The main comparison uses one text-to-SQL target and six fixed builders, with three
493   generation seeds per builder. Phase I is discovery evidence with disclosed task overlap, a proxy
494   judge, and a post-trace human control. The new statistical analysis is post-review and does not

496   pling chain. W1/W3 now share the canonical data but remain descriptive; selection uncertainty and
497   independent utility validation are outstanding. The one-seed MATH-500 free-arm probe provides
498   parallel cross-domain support: outcome diversity and one-run headroom reappear, but bare remains
499   the best fixed member. Its protocol is in Appendix H; it supplies no clone, repeat, repaired-gate, or
500   stable-complementarity test.
501   Generated code, observed outcome diversity, and reproducible additional capability should be eval-
502   uated separately. Our archived experiments expose failures at the first two levels and leave the third
503   unresolved. Reporting absolute oracle accuracy, best-fixed accuracy, admission policy, execution
504   cost, and repeat variability makes that boundary inspectable. Establishing practical value requires
505   independently validated selection gains beyond same-code repeated sampling.
