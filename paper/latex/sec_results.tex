\subsection{Code diversity without behavioral diversity: BIRD}
\label{sec:f1-collapse}
The discovery population comprises $14$ harnesses --- $12$
evolution-generated candidates plus the \bare{} and \react{} baselines
--- with one recorded execution of each harness on each of $60$ BIRD
tasks (a single-run matrix; matched repeats were not collected here)
with a frozen \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash} target. Pairing its members gives
$\binom{12}{2}=66$ generated-candidate pairs and $91$ pairs once the two
baselines are included. Every candidate had passed a code-difference check asserting
that all files were distinct with non-trivial diffs. Yet $21$ of the
$66$ generated pairs have identical correctness vectors on every task
($36$ of $91$ including the baselines), and across all $91$ pairs
source-code similarity has essentially no rank association with
outcome disagreement (Spearman $\rho=-0.010$;
Figure~\ref{fig:phenomenon}). Mean pairwise outcome disagreement is
$3.3\%$ overall ($3.8\%$ among generated candidates), union repair over
\bare{} errors is $7.7\%$, and one-run oracle headroom is $3.3$\,pp;
\react{} differs from \bare{} on zero tasks.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.60\linewidth]{figures/fig_phenomenon.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Code diversity vs.\ behavioral collapse} (BIRD
discovery population: $14$ harnesses $\times$ $60$ tasks, $91$ pairs
including \bare{} and \react{}). Each point is a harness pair; $x$ is
token-sequence code similarity and $y$ the fraction of tasks with
differing correctness. Code similarity does not predict outcome
disagreement ($\rho=-0.010$), and $36/91$ pairs are identical on all
$60$ tasks.}
\label{fig:phenomenon}
\end{figure}

Auditing execution traces separates three routes through which distinct
source code fails to produce distinct executed strategies.
\textbf{T1 --- a mechanism described but not executed} ($6/12$
generated candidates): the program makes a single model call and runs
no artifact, the same execution path as \bare{}, while its docstring
claims retrieval or self-checking machinery that is absent from the
control flow. \textbf{T2 --- a mechanism present but never triggered}
(\react{} on $10/10$ audited tasks): the error-repair branch is real
code, but the first SQL succeeds on every audited task, so the branch
never fires and the final SQL equals \bare{}; this does not show the
repair path lacks causal force under failure. \textbf{T3 --- a broken
multi-turn path} ($1/12$): an execute-inspect-repair program returns
the model's prose as the final SQL, turning \bare{}-correct items into
harness-wrong ones. The three classes ask for different tests ---
execution-path acceptance, controlled fault injection, and
executable-output validation, respectively.

A human positive control shows the apparatus can detect genuine
specialization. Four hand-written harnesses with forcibly distinct
execution paths (execute-and-repair, three-vote, schema-linking, and
hint-first reordering) under the same frozen target and judge give
oracle headroom $6.67$\,pp on the $60$ tasks and $7.28$\,pp
(95\% interval $[3.3,10.0]$) on the full $151$-item set, with each
mechanism repairing tasks the others miss. This control was authored
after inspecting the discovery traces, so its headroom is descriptive
rather than blind; the discovery population's provenance, including the
$24/151$ items overlapping protocol development and the conservative
proxy judge, is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:phase1-provenance}.

\subsection{One-run headroom in an exploratory MATH-500 population}
\label{sec:f2-headroom}
A separate exploratory study on MATH-500 uses six builders, one seed, a
mechanism-blind validity gate, and the frozen \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}
target at temperature $0$, with one execution of each admitted harness
on the same $400$ evaluation tasks. Of $48$ generation slots $35$
admitted; two reasoning-heavy builders exhausted the $32$k generation
budget on most slots, so the population under-represents reasoning-heavy
styles. This is a replication probe, not a factorial, and it collected
neither repeats nor a same-code control.

The population looks behaviorally diverse: mean pairwise outcome
disagreement is $10.6\%$ (maximum $33.0\%$), $33$ of the $36$
members (counting \bare{}) have unique correctness vectors, and members
repair $76.0\%$ of \bare{} errors. But one-run oracle coverage is
$98.50\%$ while the best \emph{fixed} member is \bare{} itself at
$93.75\%$; every generated member is individually worse
($0.6825$--$0.915$). The bare-inclusive one-run oracle headroom is thus
$4.75$\,pp: a coverage opportunity that cannot be realized by
committing to any single program before execution. This exploratory
observation motivated the separate pre-registered repeat-and-clone
study of \S\ref{sec:f3-clones}; the two studies use different
populations, estimands, and budgets, and the $4.75$\,pp is not
compared with the controlled result as a before-and-after correction.
The second-domain replication is detailed in
Appendix~\ref{app:crossdomain}.

\subsection{Same-code clones show positive apparent complementarity}
\label{sec:f3-clones}
The pre-registered collection (frozen 2026-09-15) executes a panel of
eight generated harnesses plus \bare{} against nine \emph{equal-slot}
byte-identical \bare{} clone slots, for three independent repeats each
on the same $400$ MATH-500 tasks. The panel is a salted-hash draw from
the $35$ exploratory members, with eligibility restricted to low-call
mechanisms; the evaluation tasks had been used in the one-run probe, so
this is a prospective-execution, retrospective-task study rather than
unseen-task validation. Protocol and execution records are in
Appendix~\ref{app:wp1r}.

The repeat-averaged descriptive plugin is positive on \emph{both} arms
--- $\WpVthreePlugin$\,pp on the real panel's $350$ complete tasks and
$\WpVthreeClonePlugin$\,pp on the clone arm's $383$ --- even though the
clone population has $H_{\rm stable}=0$ by construction. This is the
paper's sharpest measurement fact: same source code, same repeat
schedule, positive apparent complementarity, exactly as the known-truth
nulls predict for a maximization-based plugin.

The cross-fitted frozen-selection gain is $G_{\rm real}=\WpVthreeG$\,pp
(CI $\WpVthreeGCI$) and $G_{\rm clone}=\WpVthreeGclone$\,pp on the two
arms' own complete-task sets, so the finite-$(R,T)$ selection noise on
the clone arm is of the same order as the panel's gain. The decision
statistic $D$ is not the arithmetic difference of those arm-level
values: both gains are recomputed by cross-fitting on the $\WpVthreeNPaired{}$
tasks complete in both arms, giving $D=\WpVthreeD$\,pp (CI
$\WpVthreeDCI$, randomization $p=\WpVthreeP$). Stable member ranking
(C-rank) is \textsc{Supported}, but the complementarity decision
abstains: $\WpExclReal\%$ of real-arm tasks are incomplete, above the
frozen $10\%$ coverage gate, so C-comp is
\textsc{Insufficient~Evidence}, the interval's upper end does not
establish equivalence at the $1$\,pp margin, and the all-$0$/all-$1$
fill scenarios for excluded tasks bracket $G$ at $\WpVthreeBounds$\,pp.
The abstention is power-driven as well as coverage-driven: at $R=3$ the
instrument detects even a strong $H=17.5$\,pp two-specialist crossover
with $0\%$ power ($0.7\%$ at $R=5$, $100\%$ at $R=10,20$;
\S\ref{sec:protocol}).

Slots do not determine spend. Offline common execution-budget replay
matches post-execution oracle coverage of the dev-ranked panel against
$b$ of the $27$ same-code executions at $b\in\{3,9,15,27\}$: the panel
minus same-code differences are $+0.37, +0.03, -0.11, -0.25$\,pp, i.e.\
tied at every budget (range $-0.25$ to $+0.37$\,pp). The panel used
$3.73\times$ the clone arm's tokens ($54.17$M vs $14.51$M,
$22{,}192$ vs $10{,}800$ logical calls), so no realized cost-matched
claim is made, and no cross-arm majority-vote accuracy is reported
because the arms emit incompatible answer formats. The frozen
pre-execution selector $\pi_Z$ abstains to \bare{} on every eval task
and ties dev-fixed at $\WpBareAcc\%$ over the three repeats (the
single-run \bare{} accuracy in the exploratory study was $93.75\%$; the
higher value here is the repeat-mean on the same task set, a different
statistic, not a contradiction), while a pre-fixed random member is
materially worse ($\WpRandomDiff$\,pp, CI $\WpRandomCI$): the observed
oracle value is not realized routing value.

\subsection{An admission-policy study on BIRD}
\label{sec:f2-admission}
A separate confirmatory BIRD study crosses six independent builders with
three seeds under strategy forcing (a named mechanism from a fixed set
versus free invention) and conformance gating, giving four arms:
II-A (free, ungated), II-B (free, gated), II-C (forced, ungated), and
II-D (forced, gated). The evaluation covers
$1{,}169$ questions from nine databases never opened during
development (one disclosed $18$-item smoke-test exception), with the
official BIRD execution scorer and a pre-outcome-frozen analysis later
re-run as a versioned corrected reanalysis. The implemented gate bundles
nontrivial-mechanism structural screening with conformance verification
--- two of eight forced prompt-transformation slots cannot pass even
when neutrally valid --- so the treatment does not isolate verification
causally.
