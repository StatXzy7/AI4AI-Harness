\subsection{Execution variability and the stable-complementarity question}
Independent executions destabilize one-run comparisons: canonical R2 replay
finds \RevisionRtwoFlips/28{,}000 same-harness verdict flips
(\RevisionRtwoFlipPercent\%), and retaining all 18 cells changes the subset
D$-$A contrast by $\RevisionRtwoChange$\,pp
(95\% CI $\RevisionRtwoChangeCI$); the R3 audit (three bare-only cache-off
repeats) found 14.5\% of tasks changing bare correctness at least once
(Appendix~\ref{app:r3}). Treating three bare executions as three
different members gives a descriptive 6.50\,pp one-run oracle gap on the
same 400 tasks --- one-run headroom can arise from execution sampling, not
new capability.

Writing the per-task correct probabilities as $p_{h,x}$ makes the gap
precise: the stable headroom
$\frac1n\sum_x\max_h p_{h,x}-\max_h\frac1n\sum_x p_{h,x}$ vanishes for a
same-code clone pool despite positive one-run headroom, and its
two-action decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:identity}) shows a globally
dominant member also yields zero. The fresh clone-controlled collection
that tests exactly this follows (\S\ref{sec:wp1r}).
