The confirmatory answer to our primary question is negative: the strategy-forced,
conformance-gated protocol does not increase population headroom relative to free,
ungated generation. The point estimate is small ($-0.16$\,pp), the hierarchical
bootstrap CI comfortably spans zero, and the permutation test offers no evidence
against the null. Per the pre-specified conditional conclusions, we report this as
\emph{no clear improvement} --- an unresolved estimate, not evidence of no effect.

Two secondary observations qualify the null. First, both arms carry \emph{substantial}
headroom (A: 3.1--11.7\,pp across cells; D: 4.5--11.9\,pp): under the clean protocol,
free generation alone produces headroom-rich populations. Second, the per-cell
deltas are heterogeneous ($-2.5$ to $+2.8$\,pp), small-K gated cells trending
positive and large-free cells negative --- though the noise-floor analysis below
cautions against reading this spread structurally. Arm D repairs marginally fewer
bare errors (30.0\% vs.\ 31.3\%) while inducing more harm (24.6\% vs.\ 19.8\%).

The factorial decomposition must be read with two corrections the frozen plan
anticipated and the data now supply.

\textbf{Size confounding.} The gate admits $\sim$30\% fewer candidates (69 vs.\
C's 101 slots), and oracle headroom grows with population size, so part of the
unmatched $-0.67$\,pp is mechanical. At \textbf{matched population size}
(subsampling each ungated population to its gated arm's $K$, 200 draws), the gate
main effect shrinks to $-0.30$\,pp, 95\% CI $[-0.73, +0.11]$ --- \emph{not
distinguishable from zero}. More than half the unmatched effect was population
size.

\textbf{Multiplicity.} The three factorial contrasts form a pre-specified
Holm family. Raw permutation $p$ for the gate main effect is 0.028, but the
\textbf{Holm-adjusted} $p$ is 0.084 --- not confirmatory even before
K-matching. The unmatched CI excluding zero was never pre-specified
significance.

\textbf{Noise floor.} Three independent cache-off repeats of \bare{} (R3,
temperature 0) flip 14.5\% of task outcomes at least once (pairwise
$\sim$9.7--10.0\%; bare accuracy varies 63.8--65.8\% across repeats). The
cache-off arm replication (R2; arms A and D, 70 harnesses, 28{,}000 core
cells) flips 8.5\% of verdicts against the cached run while leaving
arm-level disagreement structure and every subset contrast unchanged
(Appendix~\ref{app:r2}): same-harness rerun disagreement is of the same
order as cross-harness disagreement, so a substantial share of measured
pairwise diversity is run noise --- and the sub-pp effects under
interpretation sit an order of magnitude below this floor.

\textbf{Headroom decomposition.} Oracle accuracy is essentially unchanged
(A .761 vs.\ D .757; Table~\ref{tab:decomp}); the unmatched headroom delta
is carried by best-fixed accuracy shifting, not by a loss of union
correctness --- ``less headroom'' means \emph{less routing elbow-room},
not a worse population.

The honest summary: neither component moved population headroom beyond
noise in either direction; we report the unmatched, K-matched, adjusted,
and decomposition views side by side and decline to interpret the residual.

\subsection{Diversity does not imply learnable routing}\label{sec:boundary}
\textbf{Open-set value prediction does not beat the best fixed harness.} A
leave-one-harness-out predictor over the Phase-I admitted populations fails
to beat the best fixed harness (DeepSeek arm $-3.4$\,pp,
Qwen $+0.9$\,pp, CI $[-5.2, +6.9]$; cross-protocol pool
$-0.9$\,pp). Apparent per-fold repair AUROC of 0.82--0.98 collapses under
database-stratified task-held-out controls (mean 0.512) --- the signal is
task memorization, not harness-value estimation. One narrow positive
survives: as an \emph{abstaining gate} on the 24-harness cross-protocol
pool, the same predictor gained $+6.9$\,pp over bare with zero
\emph{observed} induced harm --- on its own tuning tasks, so not a
deployment claim. Selective abstention may be easier than full routing;
per-item value estimation from code and task text alone remains open.

\subsection{Exploratory: what to measure, and what to select for}\label{sec:rich}
Two post-hoc analyses on the archived confirmatory cells (details in
Appendix~\ref{app:rich}) answer the two questions a measurement paper
invites. \textbf{(i) Output-space similarity carries the signal that
code-space similarity lacks:} mean final-SQL dissimilarity on
verdict-\emph{agreeing} cells correlates with outcome disagreement at
Spearman $\rho{=}0.59$ (within-arm $0.57$), where code similarity showed
none ($\rho{=}{-}0.01$) --- but richer channels are noisier channels
(rerun stability: calls 1.8\%, verdicts 8.5\%, SQL 70\%), and
forced-mechanism arms cost 39--42\%
multi-call trajectories (free arms 25--30\%) while scoring \emph{lower}
(.62 vs.\ .64). \textbf{(ii) Diversity-aware population selection
transfers:} selecting $k{=}8$ per cell to maximize dev-half oracle
coverage yields $+0.82$\,pp held-out headroom over top-by-accuracy
selection (95\% CI $[0.43, 1.31]$) and $+0.40$\,pp over random
($[0.18, 0.66]$); accuracy selection is itself worse than random --- it
buys the best \emph{member}, diversity buys \emph{complementarity}.
Normalized, the diversity-selected 8-subset retains 76\% of the full-pool
oracle headroom over the best fixed harness (accuracy selection: 73\%;
paired delta $+2.9$\,pp, CI $[-0.02, +6.08]$).
Population-level statistics transfer across the database split;
per-item value estimates do not (\S\ref{sec:boundary}).
