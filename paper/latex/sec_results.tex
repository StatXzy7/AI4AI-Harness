\subsection{Execution variability and the stable-complementarity question}
The archived R3 audit reports that 14.5\% of tasks changed bare correctness
at least once across three cache-off repeats. Canonical R2 replay finds
\RevisionRtwoFlips/28{,}000 same-harness verdict flips
(\RevisionRtwoFlipPercent\%). Retaining all 18 cells, including two with
singleton A subsets, changes subset D$-$A from
$\RevisionRtwoCached$ to $\RevisionRtwoOff$\,pp; the change is
$\RevisionRtwoChange$\,pp (95\% CI $\RevisionRtwoChangeCI$).
R2 subset headroom excludes bare because R2 did not acquire a matched bare
run. It is therefore distinct from the bare-inclusive primary estimand.
The old 16-cell comparison is reconciled in Appendix~\ref{app:r2}. These are
instability measurements, not standard errors or a detectability floor for
the mean treatment effect. They do not establish that every contrast is
unchanged, or identify what fraction of between-harness disagreement is noise.

Let $p_{h,x}$ denote the probability that harness $h$ answers task $x$
correctly across independent executions. For a pool including bare, define
\[
H_{\rm stable}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_x\max_h p_{h,x}
-\max_h\frac{1}{n}\sum_x p_{h,x}.
\]
A same-code clone-only pool has identical $p_{h,x}$ across members,
hence zero stable headroom despite potentially positive one-run headroom.
Adding distinct bare can retain baseline--harness complementarity.
Independent repeats estimate a fitted policy's value against its specified
comparator. A discovery-selected fixed member may be suboptimal: beating
it alone does not establish stable complementarity or unseen-task utility.
The current data lack a balanced, cost-matched clone comparison, so stable
added capability remains unestimated.

\subsection{Routing and exploratory selection}\label{sec:boundary}
A Phase-I leave-one-harness-out predictor failed to beat the best fixed
harness in the DeepSeek population ($-3.4$\,pp); the Qwen estimate was
$+0.9$\,pp with CI $[-5.2,+6.9]$. Database-stratified task-held-out
controls reduced mean repair AUROC to 0.512, consistent with task
memorization rather than reliable value prediction. These findings apply
to the tested predictors and splits.

\label{sec:rich}
The canonical W1 reanalysis remains descriptive. At $k=8$, with bare
retained, diversity selection versus top-accuracy selection changes
headroom by $\RevisionWoneHeadroom$\,pp: oracle changes by
$\RevisionWoneOracle$\,pp and best-fixed by $\RevisionWoneBest$\,pp.
A fixed member chosen only on development data changes by
$\RevisionWoneFixed$\,pp, with ties resolved consistently within each
selected subset. These are different estimands, not a demonstrated
routing gain. Appendix~\ref{app:rich} reports absolute values and the
limitations of this post-hoc, single-execution comparison.
