A BSTRACT
Automatically generated LLM harnesses can differ in source code, executed
mechanisms, and correctness outcomes. These differences need not imply reproducible additional capability. We examine the distinction on BIRD text-toSQL. A discovery population had 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on all evaluated outcomes despite distinct source code; trace inspection found absent mechanisms, untriggered branches, and broken multi-turn paths. A subsequent factorial study crossed strategy forcing and an admission gate over six builders and
three seeds, using 1,169 questions and the official BIRD scorer. Free generation already yielded 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. Post-review reanalysis of
the archived experiment estimates the combined intervention at −0.16 pp (95%
CI [−0.83, +0.87]), with no clear improvement. The implemented gate also excludes two allowed prompt-level strategy types, so this contrast cannot isolate
verification alone. Repeated executions show outcome instability, which a singlerun oracle can exploit without stable mechanism complementarity. We distinguish
observed headroom from stable task–harness advantages and identify the controls
needed to connect either to usable selection gains. The current evidence establishes these measurement limits; it does not yet identify stable complementarity
beyond repeated sampling.

I NTRODUCTION

An LLM harness is the program of prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps a frozen model.
Automatically generating many such programs creates an evaluation problem: what evidence shows
that a new program provides a new capability? Different source code may execute the same path;
different paths may produce the same answer; different answers on one run may reflect execution
randomness rather than a repeatable advantage.
We study this problem on BIRD text-to-SQL (Li et al., 2023). In a Phase-I discovery population, 21
of 66 generated-candidate pairs had identical correctness vectors. Including the shipped react harness and bare baseline gives 36 identical pairs out of 91. Pairwise code similarity had no observed
rank association with outcome disagreement (ρ = −0.01). Trace inspection found three distinct
situations: claimed mechanisms absent from control flow, mechanisms present but untriggered on
the inspected tasks, and broken multi-turn implementations. These observations motivate an audit
of the measurement process; they do not establish that generated harnesses generally collapse.
A subsequent pre-specified factorial study crossed strategy forcing and conformance gating over six
builders and three seeds. Its primary evaluation used 1,169 questions from nine databases outside
protocol development, with one disclosed 18-item smoke-test exception. Under this revised setting,
free ungated generation already produced 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. The Phase-I pattern
therefore did not recur, but changes in generation, judge, data, and execution prevent attribution to
any one protocol component without a controlled bridge.
The original gate also changes the admissible strategy space: two allowed single-call prompt transformations cannot pass its mechanism check. Consequently, the intervention combines mechanism
screening with verification. We preserve that experiment and report a versioned post-review reanalysis rather than treating it as a repaired-gate trial. The primary contrast remains −0.16 pp (95%

2.1

H ARNESS GENERATION FOR LLM S

A growing line of work generates task-specific scaffolding around a frozen model. TTHE evolves
text-to-SQL harnesses with an LLM proposer/judge loop (Nie et al., 2026); Self-Harness (Zhang
et al., 2026b) shows a model can improve its own harness; JIT-Agent (Zhang et al., 2026a) trains
a model to generate a harness per task at inference time; Task-CoEvolve (Miyai et al., 2026) coevolves tasks and scaffolds. A concurrent 2026 cluster makes harness evolution behavior-aware
from the verification side: HarnessLens (Xu et al., 2026) allocates rollout budget using behaviorlevel checks during evolution; HarnessFix (Chen et al., 2026) repairs harnesses from trace-grounded
diagnoses; AHE (Lin et al., 2026) verifies each proposed edit against self-declared behavioral predictions; gated semantic quality-diversity search (Luo et al., 2026) evolves populations over LLMassigned pathology descriptors; Harness Handbook (Wang et al., 2026) attacks behavior localization
and outer-loop search.
Our work is complementary but distinct in unit of analysis and question. Table 1 contrasts the two
nearest systems. HarnessLens uses behavior as a signal inside an evolution loop to allocate compute;
we ask whether the resulting population is behaviorally diverse as an outcome property, and how
it changes under generation-side interventions. HarnessBank (Luo et al., 2026) documents searchspace collapse and maintains a semantic diversity library with gated screening; our conformance
gate does not score semantics at all — it verifies mechanically, via counterfactual trace probes, that
a declared mechanism executes — and our design compares admission policies through a factorial
contrast. Because the implemented gate also excludes allowed prompt transformations, the current
experiment does not isolate verification’s causal contribution.

H ARNESS CONDITIONAL UTILITY AND ROUTING

Our candidates are complete automatically generated harnesses. We measure the population outcome structure a selector would inherit (§5.4), rather than build a selector. We diagnose execution paths and compare generation policies through a factorial whose treatment includes structural
screening; the present study does not establish stable selectable value.
2.3

B EHAVIORAL DIVERSITY IN LLM POPULATIONS

Diversity of LLM outputs has mostly been studied at the sample level (self-consistency, diverse
decoding) or the agent-policy level. The observation that syntactically distinct programs can exhibit
identical behavior echoes coverage gaps documented in program synthesis and mutation-testing
practice; our contribution is to instantiate and quantify it for AI-generated harness populations, with
a mechanism-level taxonomy.

3

P HASE I: M EASURING AND D IAGNOSING THE C OLLAPSE (D ISCOVERY )

Phase I is a discovery study: its role is to establish the phenomenon, the taxonomy, and the existence of task-side headroom, and to motivate the confirmatory design. None of its results is confirmatory: 24 of its 151 evaluation items overlapped protocol-development measurements, the human
control was designed after inspecting development traces, and its judge was a conservative proxy
(Appendix A). We report it with those limits stated.
3.1

H ARNESSES , OUTCOMES , AND THE THREE METRICS

A harness H wraps a frozen target model M : it receives the natural-language task x, issues one or
more calls to M , optionally executes intermediate artifacts (e.g. SQL), and returns a final answer
scored programmatically. With H0 the bare single-call baseline and Y ∈ {0, 1} correctness, the
harness marginal value is ∆(x, H, M ) = Y (x, H, M ) − Y (x, H0 , M ) ∈ {−1, 0, +1} (+1 repair,
−1 harness-induced harm).
We propose that harness-generation and harness-routing pipelines report three precondition metrics
on a held-out set, all computable from the outcome matrix any evaluation already produces: (i)
pairwise outcome disagreement — over all harness pairs, the fraction of tasks where correctness
differs; (ii) union repair rate — the fraction of bare errors some population member repairs; (iii)
bare-inclusive oracle headroom

3.3

T RACE TAXONOMY: WHY CODE DIVERSITY FAILS TO BECOME BEHAVIORAL DIVERSITY

Auditing execution traces (the sequence of LLM calls and artifact executions per task) decomposes
“different code” into three failure classes:
• T1 — mechanism described, not executed (6/12 candidates). The harness issues exactly one LLM call and never executes an artifact — the identical execution path as bare.
Docstrings claim retrieval, self-checking, or token caps; none exists in the control flow.
• T2 — mechanism present but untriggered (react, 10/10 audited tasks). The first SQL
executes successfully, so error repair is not triggered and the final SQL equals bare’s. This
does not show that repair feedback lacks a causal path when a failure occurs.
• T3 — broken multi-turn path (1/12). The one candidate implementing genuine multiturn repair returns prose as final SQL, converting bare-correct items into harness-wrong.

P HASE -I PROTOCOL PROBES AND THEIR LIMITS

Phase I also probed generation-side fixes — a strategy-forced free-form protocol (assigned mechanism, import/smoke acceptance) and a compiled Harness IR. Under the strategy-forced protocol
two of three builders produced populations whose point estimates exceeded the pre-specified thresholds (Qwen3.8-Flash 5.96, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 7.95 pp; the old-protocol arm missed at
4.64 pp), and per-strategy acceptance ordered with builder identity (GLM-5.3-Flash 3/8, Qwen3.8Flash 7/8, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 8/8, under the logged tool). These observations motivate
Phase II but are not confirmatory, for three disclosed reasons: the design was two nested contrasts
that cannot separate protocol from builder main effects; the early runs lack per-attempt logs, so budget equality cannot be audited; and acceptance was an import/smoke check, not the counterfactual
conformance gate of §4. Full condition details are in Appendix A.
Generation-null failures (M0). Re-running the original optimize loop with Qwen3.8-Flash and
DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp builders produced 40 candidates byte-identical to the bare baseline source. Code identity alone does not imply identical stochastic executions. This run supplies
no distinct-code population; it is reported as a distinct, more primitive failure mode — the pipeline
produced no distinct population at all. The collapse claims above rest exclusively on the genuinely
distinct-source day-1 population.

P HASE II: C ONFIRMATORY G ENERATION S TUDY (D ESIGN )

Phase II is the confirmatory study. Its protocol was frozen and committed before any confirmatory outcome was observed (one exception, disclosed as deviation D1 in the protocol changelog: an
18-item pipeline smoke test whose aggregate accuracies were observed and whose items were retained); deviations are logged in the protocol changelog (Appendix B). The frozen estimand, splits,
instruments, and analysis plan are summarized here.
4.1

D ESIGN

Arms. Two crossed factors: strategy forcing (S=1: the builder is assigned a named mechanism
from a fixed set of eight; S=0: the builder invents an improvement with no mechanism named) and
the conformance gate (G=1: the harness must pass the frozen conformance suite; G=0: neutral validity only). This yields four factorial arms, which we denote II-A (free/ungated), II-B (free/gated),
II-C (forced/ungated), II-D (forced/gated). A fifth, pre-specified secondary arm II-E (open mechanism discovery: the builder emits a self-specified behavioral contract over generic trace primitives,
gated on its own declaration) tests whether mechanism diversity requires author-supplied vocabulary at all; it is deliberately outside the factorial. These arm names are Phase-II-specific; Phase-I
conditions in §3.5 are always referred to with their descriptive names.
Implemented treatment. The original gate maps both hint guard and format guard to
a plain scenario that returns NO MECHANISM declared; admission requires PASS. Thus two
of eight forced strategy slots cannot pass even if neutrally valid. We interpret G as nontrivialmechanism screening plus conformance verification. The archived results do not identify pure verification, and matching population size cannot restore excluded types. Moreover, no format guard
candidate passed neutral validity in either forced arm (18 slots and 54 attempts per arm), before
mechanism gating. An extraction audit identifies a possible upstream failure: embedded SQL fences
can truncate otherwise valid Python (Appendix B).
Common attempt cap; unequal realized cost. Each slot has at most R=3 raw builder attempts
(same model, temperature 0.7, 16,384 token cap); generation stops at the first accepted candidate.

F ROZEN ANALYSIS PLAN

The primary estimand is the paired difference in bare-inclusive oracle headroom, H(II-D) −
H(II-A) over (builder × seed) cells, on the full 1,169-question set under the official judge — a
continuous effect with a two-sided 95% CI and paired permutation test, never a pass/fail threshold.
The Phase-I thresholds survive only as a descriptive checklist. Confirmatory secondaries (Holmcorrected, all four arms on the same core-400 items): gate main effect [(B−A) + (D−C)]/2, strategy main effect [(C−A) + (D−B)]/2, and their interaction (D−C) − (B−A) — whether strategy
differentiation requires execution fidelity to be realized. Original plan and corrected inference:
the original primary code omitted within-database task resampling and sampled tasks separately
across cells; the factorial code resampled cell summaries only. The versioned post-review analysis
uses 10,000 shared database/within-database task draws across all arms and cells, keeping builders
fixed and resampling generation seeds within builder. It does not add execution-repeat variance.
Exact sign-flip reference tests are conditional on observed tasks and on cell-contrast sign symmetry (one-sided primary; two-sided secondaries with Holm adjustment). These are not randomized
causal tests. H(P ) is not monotone in K — adding a harness that answers everything correctly
drives headroom to zero — so matched-K comparison, not monotonicity, licenses cross-population

ROUTING AND EXPLORATORY SELECTION

6

D ISCUSSION AND C ONCLUSION

What the evidence establishes. Source diversity, executed mechanisms, and one-run correctness
differences are distinct measurements. The Phase-I pattern did not recur in the revised Phase-II
setting; multiple protocol changes prevent identifying the cause. The corrected analysis of the original gate shows no clear headroom improvement. Because that gate also excludes allowed prompt
strategies, it does not identify the effect of verification alone.
What remains unidentified. Same-code reruns can disagree and create oracle gains. Outcome flip
rates measure instability, not the standard error of a treatment contrast, and cannot define a numerical noise floor for that contrast. A matched-cost clone control, independent repeat validation, and
validated calibration across mechanism types (Appendix G) are needed before attributing headroom
to stable additional capability.
The post-review provider acquisition attempts did not yield a complete common pool: transport interruptions left completed responses, unclosed requests, and unstarted attempts in separate auditable
states. These records are retained for provenance and missingness accounting, but are excluded
from claims about population performance, cost, or stable complementarity. Thus, the manuscript
does not use provider availability or partial acquisition as evidence for the unresolved clone-control
question.
Limitations. The main comparison uses one text-to-SQL target and six fixed builders, with three
generation seeds per builder. Phase I is discovery evidence with disclosed task overlap, a proxy

Re-selection does not establish interval coverage. An exact counterexample rules out a universal 95% coverage guarantee for our proposed positive-weight re-selection percentile interval. Let
nine independent databases each contain one Bernoulli(p) task state. Sixteen fixed candidates comprise eight state-0 specialists followed by eight state-1 specialists; bare is always wrong. Apply the

1 − p9 − (1 − p)9 , or 61.26% at p = 0.1. Averaging identical cells or overlapping fixed splits does
