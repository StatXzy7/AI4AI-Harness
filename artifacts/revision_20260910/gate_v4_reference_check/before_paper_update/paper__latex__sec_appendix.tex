\section{Phase-I Provenance and Conditions (Detail)}\label{app:phase1-provenance}

\paragraph{Discovery status.} Phase I (Sections~\ref{sec:phase1} and
\ref{sec:boundary}) is a discovery study. 24 of its 151 evaluation items
overlapped day-1 protocol-development measurements; the human control was
designed after inspecting day-1 traces (its observed headroom is descriptive,
not a blind estimate of stable complementarity); the repository history was initialized
retrospectively, and the decision-log changelog is the authoritative
ordering record. Phase-I admission thresholds (union repair
$\geq$15\%, headroom $\geq$5\,pp, $\geq$3 pairwise-distinct non-empty fix
sets) were frozen in the internal decision log before the 151-item
evaluation; the v6$\rightarrow$v7 threshold change (20\%$\rightarrow$15\%)
is disclosed, and both readings admit the same populations. This is an
internal pre-evaluation freeze, not external preregistration.

\paragraph{Data hygiene.} The bare baseline flipped 3 of 60 items between
the two day-1 runs (API nondeterminism at temperature 0), so 60-item
columns are conditioned on slightly different bare-error sets. Phase-I
judging used a set-of-rows-equality proxy that we later measured to be
uniformly conservative relative to the official BIRD scorer (on the two
Phase-I matrices with logged final SQL, it never credits an answer the
official scorer rejects; agreement 0.97--0.99).

\paragraph{Phase-I populations.} Seed baselines: \texttt{bare} (single
call) and \texttt{react} (execution feedback loop, shipped with TTHE).
\emph{Old proposer protocol}: six candidates selected from TTHE's
optimize-loop output (GLM-5.3-Flash builder), selected for behavioral
diversity using only the 36 questions disjoint from the evaluation set, by a
rule frozen before evaluation. \emph{Strategy-forced free-form}:
GLM-5.3-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp builders,
eight assigned mechanisms each; the GLM population
is the logged re-run (K=3; the original 09-01 run recorded zero accepted
outputs but its tool was not preserved and the observation is unaudited).
\emph{Harness IR}: six harnesses compiled deterministically from
declarative mechanism specifications (context $\in$
\{plain, schema\_link, hint\_guard, link+hint\}, candidate generation with
temperature, per-candidate execution, repair rounds, selection rule), so a
declared mechanism is present in the control flow by construction.
\emph{Human positive control}: the four hand-written harnesses of
\S\ref{sec:positive}.

\paragraph{Generation-provenance gaps.} The 09-01 free-form populations
were produced in-session; their generation tool was not preserved, and
per-attempt logs do not exist for them --- so budget equality cannot be
audited for those runs, and we do not claim it. Their acceptance was an
import/smoke check at evaluation time, \emph{not} the Phase-II
counterfactual conformance gate. The tool of record from 2026-09-03
(\texttt{builder\_generate3.py}) logs every attempt (3 retries,
16{,}384-token budget, per-attempt stage and finish reason); the completed
per-strategy grids and the builder-acceptance ordering (3/8, 7/8, 8/8)
come from this logged tool and are post-hoc with respect to the frozen
admission decisions.

\section{Phase-II Protocol Summary and Deviation Log}\label{app:phase2-protocol}

rules, and the historical deviation changelog) is
\texttt{PHASE2\_PROTOCOL.md} in the code release, frozen at the commit
recorded in \texttt{PROTOCOL\_FREEZE.txt}. Summary: $2{\times}2$ factorial
plus open-mechanism arm; six builders $\times$ three seeds; at most
$R{=}3$ attempts per slot, stopping at first admission, with no top-up;
contamination registry (fail-closed,
two development databases banned wholesale); 1{,}169-question confirmatory

official BIRD judge with enforced 30\,s timeout; response cache keyed on

D$-$A on paired (builder $\times$ seed) cells with hierarchical bootstrap
and permutation inference; Holm-corrected factorial secondaries on the
common core; K-matched sensitivity only. Deviations D1--D13 include the
disclosed 18-item pipeline smoke test, instrument-calibration fixes made
before any confirmatory outcome was observed, the credential-handling

each with its rationale and date in the changelog.
The post-review corrections are recorded in
\texttt{review-stage/REVISION\_20260910.md}; revised results do not replace
the historical freeze. In particular, the implemented gate structurally

\paragraph{Upstream code-extraction audit.}\label{app:extraction}
Across the 36 forced C/D cells, all 108 \texttt{format\_guard} attempts
failed neutral validity: 71 logs report unterminated Python strings,
36 report no Python fence, and one reports a provider timeout.
The archived generator's non-greedy Python-fence extractor terminates at
the first subsequent triple backtick, including one inside a Python
string. Three synthetic, syntactically valid files containing SQL fences
in an ordinary string, docstring, or regex become invalid prefixes;
the extractor nevertheless sets its truncation flag to false.
This extractor is unchanged from the review-locked commit, but the historical attempt
logs do not bind its execution-time source hash. Full builder messages
were not saved by this generator. Thus the failure pattern is compatible
with extraction damage, not proof that all historical failures had this
cause or that repaired candidates would pass admission.
We found 74 saved extracted files, all syntactically invalid; three
coexist with no-fence logs and are retained as provenance conflicts,
not attributed to those attempts. These findings place a generation
interface failure alongside the gate's explicit strategy exclusions.

\section{Exploratory Fingerprint and Selection Analyses}\label{app:rich}
Both analyses are post-hoc on the archived confirmatory cells (18
builder$\times$seed cells, 384 admitted candidates, core-400 outcomes).
The versioned \texttt{experiment/revision/} scripts now use the same
canonical loader, frozen task list, membership and source checks as the
main reanalysis. They remain descriptive: these tasks had already been
analyzed, and this is not independent transfer validation.

\paragraph{Rich fingerprints.} To isolate the loader change, the W3
reanalysis retains the legacy lossy SQL text transform: semicolon deletion,
backtick replacement, lowercasing even segments after splitting on single
quotes, and whitespace joining. This is not a SQL parser or semantic
normalization; it can alter literals and quoted identifiers. Among
$\RevisionWthreeSameSQL$ pair--task observations with two identical,
nonempty transformed SQL strings, $\RevisionWthreeSameDisagreements$
have different verdicts. This is an observed consistency check, not a
property guaranteed by construction.

Pair-level SQL dissimilarity averages $(1-$token-Jaccard$)$ only over
verdict-agreeing tasks with two nonempty SQL strings. Its Spearman
correlation with overall outcome disagreement is $\RevisionWthreeRho$
across 4{,}386 within-cell pairs and $\RevisionWthreeWithinRho$ across
1{,}010 within-arm pairs. Pairs share candidates and tasks, and the SQL
metric conditions on verdict agreement; these correlations have no
independence-based significance or causal interpretation.

In R2's 70-harness first cache-off acquisition, call counts change on
$\RevisionWthreeCallChanges/28000$ comparisons and verdicts on
$\RevisionRtwoFlips/28000$. Transformed SQL changes on
$\RevisionWthreeSQLChanges/\RevisionWthreeSQLN$ comparisons
($\RevisionWthreeSQLPercent\%$); 64 comparisons lack two nonempty SQL
strings and are excluded only from the SQL denominator. The mean token
Jaccard among changed SQL pairs is $\RevisionWthreeJaccard$. These
channel-specific rates do not define an effect-size noise floor.
Canonical logical-call costs are reported separately in Table~\ref{tab:cost}.

\paragraph{Diversity-aware selection.} Each cell pools all admitted
candidates across arms. The reanalysis retains the legacy seed, 100
random database-half splits, 200 random subset draws per split and cell,
and candidate-selection rules. It records a new deterministic schedule:
the historical set-derived cell order was not archived, so identical
historical splits and draws cannot be recovered from the seed alone.
Selection uses development databases; evaluation uses the complementary
databases. The overlapping splits are not 1{,}800 independent experiments.
Methods are top-$k$ development accuracy, uniform random subsets, greedy
development oracle coverage, and greedy mean accuracy plus
$\lambda$ times oracle coverage for $\lambda\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$.
All candidate choices are frozen within a split before its evaluation.

Table~\ref{tab:selection} retains the common AD bare in every evaluated
subset; $k$ counts candidates only. The JSON also records candidate-only
values to distinguish the historical estimand. Oracle and best-fixed use
held-out outcomes as descriptive upper bounds. Dev-fixed instead chooses
one member using development accuracy, with ascending member-name ties
and bare first. Deterministic methods already retain a globally dev-best
candidate; differences in this comparator may arise from which tied
maxima survive, not improved maximum development accuracy.

At $k=8$, div-only versus top-acc changes oracle by

\section{Worked Examples from the Phase-I Trace Audit}\label{app:examples}

To make the failure taxonomy of \S\ref{sec:taxonomy} concrete, this section
walks one BIRD task through the audited harnesses. Artifacts are verbatim
from the day-1 trace audit (5 harnesses $\times$ 10 tasks); its logger caps
stored SQL excerpts at 120 characters, so truncations are marked
\emph{[trunc.]}. Full harness sources and traces are in the code release.

\paragraph{The task.} BIRD dev-set question 352 (\texttt{card\_games}
database; question text verbatim, the typo is the dataset's):
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
Calculate the percentage of the cards availabe in Chinese Simplified.
\end{quote}
Its BIRD hint:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
'Chinese Simplified' is the language; percentage = Divide(Sum(id where
language = 'Chinese Simplified'), Count(id)) *100
\end{quote}
Gold SQL (executing the two on the released database yields 8.7734 and
8.7728 respectively --- the official scorer separates them):
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
SELECT CAST(SUM(CASE WHEN T2.language = 'Chinese Simplified' THEN 1 ELSE 0
END) AS REAL) * 100 / COUNT(T1.id) FROM cards AS T1 INNER JOIN foreign\_data
AS T2 ON T1.uuid = T2.uuid
\end{quote}
The \bare{} harness answers:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN language = 'Chinese Simplified' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) *
100.0 / COUNT(id) AS percentage FROM foreign\_data;
\end{quote}
marked wrong by the official scorer: it counts directly over
\texttt{foreign\_data}, whereas the gold query counts after joining to
\texttt{cards}; the hint does not spell out that join.

\paragraph{\react{} (T2: mechanism present but untriggered).} \react's control
flow does execute SQL and retries on database errors. But its first call is
byte-identical to \bare's prompt, and here the generated SQL executes without
error, so the error-triggered retry never fires and the final SQL equals
\bare's. Across all 10 audited tasks the pattern held: SQL executions
present, final SQL byte-identical to \bare's on 10/10 --- the feedback loop
is real code whose repair branch did not activate on these tasks. Controlled
failure injection is needed to test whether feedback changes the output.

\paragraph{\texttt{cand\_bird\_g1\_b0r1\_g0} (code-level diversity,
behavioral identity).} This candidate is a genuinely different program: it
wraps the single coder call in a daemon-thread watchdog and fires one hedged
duplicate re-ask if the call stalls past 40\,s. The mechanism exists in the
control flow --- but its trigger (a stalled API call) never occurred in the
audit: zero SQL executions, and the final SQL equaled \bare's on all 10
audited tasks. Code difference without behavioral difference survives even
when the added mechanism is real: it simply never fires.

\paragraph{\texttt{cand\_bird\_g1\_b1r1\_g1} (T3: a broken multi-turn
path).} The one audited candidate implementing genuine execute-inspect-repair
returns the model's \emph{prose} as final SQL. On a task \bare{} answered
correctly (``State the alternative languages available for card named Annul
numbered 29.''), its logged final SQL begins, verbatim:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
with "only a single read-only SELECT is allowed". The previous "query" was
actually just prose text (the model wrote rea\emph{[trunc.]}
\end{quote}
The repair re-prompt invites the failure: asked to fix a query that ``was
actually just prose'', the frozen target responds with more prose reasoning,
no SQL block is found, and the reasoning text is submitted as the query. The
item flips from bare-correct to harness-wrong.

authored controls, separate from the controls used to develop its probes.
The instrument was fixed before a separate model received the public
contracts and callable interface, without its implementation or earlier
controls. The author supplied two intended conforming and two intended
nonconforming implementations for each of the eight mechanism types.
Control source, author-reference labels, instrument, measurement code,
and execution order were hashed before the first evaluation. No benchmark
or provider generation was run, and this first measurement is retained unchanged.

The nine review-required cases were retained. A third model received the
full control source and traces without author-reference labels or
automatic verdicts, for source-contract review of all 32 controls and
semantic adjudication of the four designated mechanism types.
Independent source review supported conformance for one decomposition
control and two error-classification controls that the instrument rejected.
The decomposition stub returned the payload of the first plan step whose
text occurred in a request; retaining the earlier step in the context of
the next request therefore retrieved the earlier payload again. The
classification check removed the exact error text but still counted
an ordinary \texttt{Schema:} heading as a category, making the extracted
category set disagree with the expected error class. These are instrument
failures on these controls, not observed failures of a target model.
Three author-intended positive controls were disputed: two schema-linking
controls retained an unrelated table, and one two-view control batched
its requests and mishandled an empty result. Their original labels and
the review disagreements were retained separately. Among the 29 controls
whose author labels and source review agreed, the instrument rejected
3 of 13 conforming controls; all 16 nonconforming controls were rejected
after the designated semantic review. The instrument therefore did not
pass this finite control check. These counts do not estimate error rates
in a generated population.
Semantic adjudication of the designated 16 controls yielded 7 conforming,
8 nonconforming, and 1 indeterminate case. The indeterminate case already
had a structural failure, which was retained without resolving its semantics.
This small, deliberately balanced control set is not a random sample of
generated harnesses. Passing it would not validate the production
interface or replace the common-raw-pool comparison of admission policies.
The source, frozen inputs, full traces, and adjudications are retained
in the accompanying revision artifacts.

\paragraph{Revised instrument and a new independent control set.}
Gate-v3 corrected decomposition request attribution and treated category
keywords and shared request templates as review clues rather than automatic
rejections. We fixed this version before fresh model contexts authored and
reviewed a new set of 32 controls, again two intended positive and two intended
negative controls per mechanism. The review packet withheld author labels,
automatic verdicts, and instrument-derived attribution metadata. All 32
controls received source review; the 20 controls in the four prompt/formulation
groups plus decomposition also received semantic or data-flow review.
Structural outcomes were 8 passes, 10 failures, and 14 review-required cases,
with no execution failures. Following one recorded evidence clarification,
source review classified 15 controls as conforming and 17 as nonconforming;
semantic review classified 8 as conforming, 11 as nonconforming, and 1 as
indeterminate.

The indeterminate classification control requested an intermediate error
category, but the synthetic interface returned SQL text. Its fallback then
selected a semantics repair action. This establishes a response-type mismatch
in the fixture, not a real solver's classification error. A second classification
control was reference-disputed: its source mapped the supplied schema-failure
message to a semantics action. Among the 31 undisputed references, 14 of 15
conforming controls were accepted and one remained unresolved; all 16
nonconforming controls were rejected. The prespecified finite check therefore
remained unmet because of the unresolved case and insufficient agreed positive
coverage for error classification. One voting control's conformance applied
only to result arbitration; it did not establish task-conditioned candidate
generation. These finite mechanism checks do not validate complete harness
operation or replace the planned common-pool policy comparison.

\paragraph{Runtime response contracts.}
We subsequently tested seven archived ungated, strategy-forced candidates,
selecting the first available filename per strategy rather than by performance;
no format-guard candidate was available (Appendix~\ref{app:extraction}).
The 27 synthetic scenarios exposed both candidate and instrument limitations.
The two-view candidate skipped its second execution when the first result was
nonempty, contrary to its original ``execute both'' declaration. In contrast,
the schema-linking candidate requested JSON but received plain text, and
the full plan in decomposition requests prevented the frozen probe from
uniquely attributing the current target.
We retained those observations and tested source-assigned response profiles
in 11 further development scenarios. Dedicated subquestion fields identified
the current target, executable SQL payloads reached the final assembly, and
four errors produced by an in-memory SQLite database elicited the corresponding
syntax or schema repair instructions. Schema-linking JSON changed the explicit
table/column restrictions in the SQL-writing request, although the full schema
remained present. Retaining schema context therefore cannot by itself establish
that the linked subset was ignored. These exposed-candidate checks distinguish
response-interface failures from observable candidate deviations; they do not
replace independent calibration or provide revised benchmark outcomes.
