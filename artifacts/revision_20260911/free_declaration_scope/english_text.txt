009                                              A BSTRACT
010
011            Automatically generated LLM harnesses can differ in source code, executed
012            mechanisms, and correctness outcomes. These differences need not imply re-
013            producible additional capability. We examine the distinction on BIRD text-to-
014            SQL. A discovery population had 36 of 91 harness pairs identical on all evalu-
015            ated outcomes despite distinct source code; trace inspection found absent mech-
016
               anisms, untriggered branches, and broken multi-turn paths. A subsequent facto-
               rial study crossed strategy forcing and an admission gate over six builders and
017
               three seeds, using 1,169 questions and the official BIRD scorer. Free genera-
018
               tion already yielded 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. Post-review reanalysis of
019            the archived experiment estimates the combined intervention at −0.16 pp (95%
020            CI [−0.83, +0.87]), with no clear improvement. The implemented gate also ex-
021            cludes two allowed prompt-level strategy types, so this contrast cannot isolate
022            verification alone. Repeated executions show outcome instability, which a single-
023            run oracle can exploit without stable mechanism complementarity. We distinguish
024            observed headroom from stable task–harness advantages and identify the controls
025            needed to connect either to usable selection gains. The current evidence estab-
026            lishes these measurement limits; it does not yet identify stable complementarity
027
               beyond repeated sampling.

029   1   I NTRODUCTION
030
031   An LLM harness is the program of prompts, tools, and control flow that wraps a frozen model.
032   Automatically generating many such programs creates an evaluation problem: what evidence shows
033   that a new program provides a new capability? Different source code may execute the same path;
034   different paths may produce the same answer; different answers on one run may reflect execution
035   randomness rather than a repeatable advantage.
036   We study this problem on BIRD text-to-SQL (Li et al., 2023). In a Phase-I discovery population, 21
037   of 66 generated-candidate pairs had identical correctness vectors. Including the shipped react har-
038   ness and bare baseline gives 36 identical pairs out of 91. Pairwise code similarity had no observed
039   rank association with outcome disagreement (ρ = −0.01). Trace inspection found three distinct
040   situations: claimed mechanisms absent from control flow, mechanisms present but untriggered on
041   the inspected tasks, and broken multi-turn implementations. These observations motivate an audit
042   of the measurement process; they do not establish that generated harnesses generally collapse.
043   A subsequent pre-specified factorial study crossed strategy forcing and conformance gating over six
044   builders and three seeds. Its primary evaluation used 1,169 questions from nine databases outside
045   protocol development, with one disclosed 18-item smoke-test exception. Under this revised setting,
046   free ungated generation already produced 8.6 pp observed oracle headroom. The Phase-I pattern
047   therefore did not recur, but changes in generation, judge, data, and execution prevent attribution to
048   any one protocol component without a controlled bridge.
049   The original gate also changes the admissible strategy space: two allowed single-call prompt trans-
050   formations cannot pass its mechanism check. Consequently, the intervention combines mechanism
051   screening with verification. We preserve that experiment and report a versioned post-review reanal-
052   ysis rather than treating it as a repaired-gate trial. The primary contrast remains −0.16 pp (95%
053   CI [−0.83, +0.87]). This unresolved estimate neither demonstrates improvement nor establishes
      equivalence.

068   Our contribution at this stage is an empirical account of three measurement boundaries:
069
070         1. Code, trace, and outcome diversity differ. Discovery examples show why an inactive
071            branch and a missing mechanism require different tests.
072         2. Admission and inference define the comparison. A factorial study and an explicit cor-
073            rection of its data and statistical chain show which intervention was actually evaluated and
074            what its estimate supports.
075         3. Observed oracle value is not stable or usable value. Repeat instability and ex-
076            ploratory selection results motivate separate reporting of oracle accuracy, best-fixed ac-
077            curacy, repeated-run advantages, and realized selector performance.
078
079   The decisive remaining question is whether a real harness population outperforms an equal-budget
080   population of independent executions of the same code, with advantages that survive independent
081   repeats. The current study has not completed that control. We therefore treat stable complementarity
082   as an estimand to be tested, not a discovered property.

088   2.1   H ARNESS GENERATION FOR LLM S
089
090   A growing line of work generates task-specific scaffolding around a frozen model. TTHE evolves
091   text-to-SQL harnesses with an LLM proposer/judge loop (Nie et al., 2026); Self-Harness (Zhang
092   et al., 2026b) shows a model can improve its own harness; JIT-Agent (Zhang et al., 2026a) trains
093
      a model to generate a harness per task at inference time; Task-CoEvolve (Miyai et al., 2026) co-
      evolves tasks and scaffolds. A concurrent 2026 cluster makes harness evolution behavior-aware
094
      from the verification side: HarnessLens (Xu et al., 2026) allocates rollout budget using behavior-
095
      level checks during evolution; HarnessFix (Chen et al., 2026) repairs harnesses from trace-grounded
096   diagnoses; AHE (Lin et al., 2026) verifies each proposed edit against self-declared behavioral pre-
097   dictions; gated semantic quality-diversity search (Luo et al., 2026) evolves populations over LLM-
098   assigned pathology descriptors; Harness Handbook (Wang et al., 2026) attacks behavior localization
099   and outer-loop search.
100
      Our work is complementary but distinct in unit of analysis and question. Table 1 contrasts the two
101
      nearest systems. HarnessLens uses behavior as a signal inside an evolution loop to allocate compute;
102   we ask whether the resulting population is behaviorally diverse as an outcome property, and how
103   it changes under generation-side interventions. HarnessBank (Luo et al., 2026) documents search-
104   space collapse and maintains a semantic diversity library with gated screening; our conformance
105   gate does not score semantics at all — it verifies mechanically, via counterfactual trace probes, that
106   a declared mechanism executes — and our design compares admission policies through a factorial
107   contrast. Because the implemented gate also excludes allowed prompt transformations, the current
      experiment does not isolate verification’s causal contribution.

      2.2   H ARNESS CONDITIONAL UTILITY AND ROUTING
109
110   Harness quality is instance-conditional: Gupta et al. (2026) and Cho (2026) document that no single
111   harness dominates across instances. HELIX evaluates a full sibling-harness outcome matrix and
112   portfolio complementarity beyond the best fixed candidate, listing a router as future work (Fan &
113   Huang, 2026). STS routes over six fixed human-designed scaffolds (Zhou et al., 2026); GRASP
114   gates skill edits into a development-time library (Moll et al., 2026); Kim (2026) learn instance-level
115   routing over {no-search, search, abstain}; Liu et al. (2026) route models within a harness. Classical
116
      algorithm selection (Rice, 1976) and portfolio solvers such as SATzilla (Xu et al., 2008; Kotthoff,
      2014) select enumerated algorithms from instance features.
117
118   Our candidates are complete automatically generated harnesses. We measure the population out-
119   come structure a selector would inherit (§5.4), rather than build a selector. We diagnose execu-
120   tion paths and compare generation policies through a factorial whose treatment includes structural
121   screening; the present study does not establish stable selectable value.
122
123   2.3   B EHAVIORAL DIVERSITY IN LLM POPULATIONS
124
      Diversity of LLM outputs has mostly been studied at the sample level (self-consistency, diverse
125
      decoding) or the agent-policy level. The observation that syntactically distinct programs can exhibit
126
      identical behavior echoes coverage gaps documented in program synthesis and mutation-testing
127   practice; our contribution is to instantiate and quantify it for AI-generated harness populations, with
128   a mechanism-level taxonomy.

139
140   A harness H wraps a frozen target model M : it receives the natural-language task x, issues one or
141   more calls to M , optionally executes intermediate artifacts (e.g. SQL), and returns a final answer
142   scored programmatically. With H0 the bare single-call baseline and Y ∈ {0, 1} correctness, the
143   harness marginal value is ∆(x, H, M ) = Y (x, H, M ) − Y (x, H0 , M ) ∈ {−1, 0, +1} (+1 repair,
144   −1 harness-induced harm).
145   We propose that harness-generation and harness-routing pipelines report three precondition metrics
146   on a held-out set, all computable from the outcome matrix any evaluation already produces: (i)
147   pairwise outcome disagreement — over all harness pairs, the fraction of tasks where correctness
148   differs; (ii) union repair rate — the fraction of bare errors some population member repairs; (iii)
149   bare-inclusive oracle headroom

      3.3   T RACE TAXONOMY: WHY CODE DIVERSITY FAILS TO BECOME BEHAVIORAL DIVERSITY
195
196   Auditing execution traces (the sequence of LLM calls and artifact executions per task) decomposes
197   “different code” into three failure classes:
198
199          • T1 — mechanism described, not executed (6/12 candidates). The harness issues ex-
200            actly one LLM call and never executes an artifact — the identical execution path as bare.
201            Docstrings claim retrieval, self-checking, or token caps; none exists in the control flow.
202          • T2 — mechanism present but untriggered (react, 10/10 audited tasks). The first SQL
203            executes successfully, so error repair is not triggered and the final SQL equals bare’s. This
204
               does not show that repair feedback lacks a causal path when a failure occurs.
             • T3 — broken multi-turn path (1/12). The one candidate implementing genuine multi-
205
               turn repair returns prose as final SQL, converting bare-correct items into harness-wrong.
206
207   The taxonomy is actionable: T1 demands an execution-path acceptance gate, T2 demands controlled
208   faults to test trigger and feedback use, T3 demands executable-output validation — all generation-
209   protocol requirements.

257
      lary at all; it is deliberately outside the factorial. These arm names are Phase-II-specific; Phase-I
258   conditions in §3.5 are always referred to with their descriptive names.

260

264   cation, and matching population size cannot restore excluded types. Moreover, no format guard
265   candidate passed neutral validity in either forced arm (18 slots and 54 attempts per arm), before
266   mechanism gating. An extraction audit identifies a possible upstream failure: embedded SQL fences

462   6     D ISCUSSION AND C ONCLUSION
463
464   What the evidence establishes. Source diversity, executed mechanisms, and one-run correctness
465   differences are distinct measurements. The Phase-I pattern did not recur in the revised Phase-II
466   setting; multiple protocol changes prevent identifying the cause. The corrected analysis of the orig-
467   inal gate shows no clear headroom improvement. Because that gate also excludes allowed prompt
468   strategies, it does not identify the effect of verification alone.
469   What remains unidentified. Same-code reruns can disagree and create oracle gains. Outcome flip
470   rates measure instability, not the standard error of a treatment contrast, and cannot define a numer-
471   ical noise floor for that contrast. A matched-cost clone control, independent repeat validation, and
472   validated calibration across mechanism types (Appendix G) are needed before attributing headroom
473   to stable additional capability.
474   Limitations. The main comparison uses one text-to-SQL target and six fixed builders, with three
475   generation seeds per builder. Phase I is discovery evidence with disclosed task overlap, a proxy
476   judge, and a post-trace human control. The new statistical analysis is post-review and does not add
477   independent executions. K-matched and R2 results now share the corrected data and resampling
478   chain. W1/W3 now share the canonical data but remain descriptive; selection uncertainty and in-
479   dependent utility validation are outstanding. The completed one-seed MATH-500 free-arm probe
480   (Appendix H) extends the observed outcome comparison to a second domain, but does not supply a
481   clone control, a repaired-gate factorial, or an independent test of stable complementarity.
482   Generated code, observed outcome diversity, and reproducible additional capability should be eval-
483   uated separately. Our archived experiments expose failures at the first two levels and leave the third
484   unresolved. Reporting absolute oracle accuracy, best-fixed accuracy, admission policy, execution
485   cost, and repeat variability makes that boundary inspectable. Establishing practical value requires
      independently validated selection gains beyond same-code repeated sampling.
